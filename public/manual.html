Getting Started

What RetailPOS is, how to log in, and how the app is organized.

Logging in

Open the app in a browser and sign in with your username and password. On a brand-new install, the default administrator account is username admin, password 123456 — you'll be forced to change this password on first login.

Security notePasswords are stored and checked in plain text and there is no session timeout beyond closing the browser. Don't reuse a sensitive password for the admin account, and change the default immediately on a new installation.

The sidebar

The left sidebar is grouped into hubs — Sales, Accounts, Warehouse, Purchasing, Dispatch, and Admin — plus Dashboard at the top. Clicking a hub opens a tile grid of the individual screens inside it; which tiles you see depends on your permissions (see Security Groups). This manual link itself opens in a new tab so you can keep it open for reference while you work.

Branches

Everything in the app — stock, sales, drawers, warehouse bins — is scoped to a branch. If your account has multi-branch access and is assigned to more than one branch, you'll see a branch switcher; otherwise you're locked to your assigned default branch. See Branches for how locations are set up.

Permissions

What you can see and do is controlled by the Security Group your account belongs to (Administrator, Manager, Cashier, or a custom group your admin created). If a button or section seems to be missing, it's most likely a permissions issue — ask an administrator to check your group under Security Groups.

Dashboard

The landing page after login — a snapshot of how the business (or your branch) is doing right now.

Everyone sees a dashboard regardless of role. It shows:

  • Today's and this month's sales totals and transaction counts
  • Total active customers
  • A low-stock count badge — also shown as a red notification in the top header everywhere in the app, so it's always visible, not just on this page
  • The five most recent transactions
  • A 7-day sales trend
  • A per-branch breakdown, if you have multi-branch access
Low stockThe low-stock count checks each product's stock at the branch level first, falling back to its overall stock figure only for products that have never been tracked per-branch. Rental items and services are never counted as "low stock" since they don't carry ordinary sale inventory.
Salesperson viewAn employee flagged Salesperson (see Employees) sees a "My Sales Only" Dashboard scoped to their own transactions — today's/month's totals, recent transactions, and the 7-day trend all cover just their own sales, and the per-branch breakdown is left off entirely. The same personal scoping applies to Transactions, CRM, and Commissions.

Layout

The sidebar and top header stay visible in POS, so you can navigate away to another section at any time — an exit icon at the top-left of the register screen is also available as a shortcut back to the Dashboard. The ticket table on the left lists items on the current sale; the button grid on the right groups every register action (lookups, discounts, hold/quote, void/cancel, payment) into one place, similar to a dedicated terminal.

Opening a sale

The first thing you'll see on entering POS is a prompt to open a cash drawer — you can skip it, but a drawer session lets the app track cash in/out for reconciliation later (see Cash Drawers). Type or scan an item's SKU/barcode into the item-number box (top right) and press Enter to add it directly. To ring up many of the same item at once, type the quantity, a *, then the item — e.g. 250*HAMMER-16 adds 250 hammers (needs pos_change_quantity; it's refused if there isn't that much stock). Part quantities like 12.5*ROPE work for measured units (yards, kg…) and services. Or use Item Lookup (needs pos_item_lookup) on the button grid to browse/search the catalog by name and click items in (retail items only — rentals are managed on their own screen). Click a ticket line to select it, then use Item Zoom (view/edit that product, also pos_item_lookup) or Quantity (pos_change_quantity) on the grid — quantity can also be changed inline on the row itself, either with +/− or by typing the new quantity into the row's quantity box and pressing Enter (typing needs pos_change_quantity; 0 removes the line, Esc cancels, and a quantity over the stock on hand or a part quantity on a whole-unit item is refused and the box goes back to what it was), and the row's × removes the line. The Salesperson dropdown next to the customer defaults to the cashier; pick the salesperson who made the sale (any active employee flagged Salesperson) and it counts toward their targets instead — the choice is kept when the ticket is held and recalled, and shows on the receipt. A ticket started by a salesperson — one they held for the cashier to finish, a quote of theirs loaded into the POS, or a sale they're ringing up themselves — always belongs to them: the dropdown is locked to their name and the cashier can't hand it to anyone else (the server applies the same rule). A sales manager can still change who a past sale is credited to from Transactions. Attach a customer via Cust Lookup (needs pos_customer_lookup) to accrue loyalty points, apply store credit, trigger a customer's discount card, or sell on a credit account — type part of a name, phone, email or customer number and the matching customers appear as you type (the first 50; keep typing to narrow a common name down); Cust Zoom (also pos_customer_lookup) opens that customer's history once one is attached. If the customer isn't on file yet, + Add New Customer in the Cust Lookup window (needs customers_add) opens the normal customer form — pre-filled with whatever was typed in the search box — and saving attaches the new customer straight to the ticket without leaving POS. If they turn out to match an existing customer, the duplicate warning offers Use This Customer to attach that one instead. Each button grid action is omitted entirely — not just disabled — for a cashier whose security group lacks its permission.

Discounts, promotions, and discount cards

Open Ticket Discount on the button grid for the discount/promo entry:

  • Manual discount — a cashier with the pos_discounts permission can apply a cart discount directly, as a dollar amount ($) or a percentage of the ticket (%, picked beside the amount). A percentage is of the ticket after any line discounts and keeps following the ticket — add an item and the discount grows with it — and the totals show it as Discount (10%); a dollar amount stays fixed. Either is capped by the store's Maximum Discount % (Settings).
  • Promo codes — type a code at checkout; it's validated against active date windows, usage limits, and minimum-purchase rules before it's applied.
  • Auto-applied promotions — any active, code-less promotion that matches the cart is offered automatically (see Promotions).
  • Discount cards — attaching a customer who carries an active discount card applies their flat percentage automatically, and it always takes priority over a promo code — attaching such a customer clears any promo already on the cart (see Discount Cards).
  • Customer Category discount — if the customer has no discount card, their Customer Category discount (if any) applies the same way instead — a discount card still wins if the customer somehow has both.

Whichever kind it is, the ticket discount comes off before tax: it's spread across the lines in proportion to their amounts and each line is taxed on what's left. For example, $1,000 of goods at 15% GCT with a $100 discount is $900 + $135 tax = $1,035. The totals show Subtotal, Discount, Tax, then Amount Due, and the receipt follows the same order. The same rule applies to a quote's Discount and to a rental quote's discount (which never reduces the refundable deposit). A return credit on an exchange is different — it's money the customer already paid, tax included — so like Available Credit and Cash Back it still comes off after tax. Returning an item from a discounted sale refunds what was actually paid for it: its price less its share of the ticket discount, plus its tax.

Line-item discounts

The % button on a cart row (needs pos_discounts) opens a per-line discount, separate from the whole-ticket Ticket Discount above. An item is only eligible if it isn't already covered by an active promotion (no stacking a manual discount on a promo) and its brand isn't on the store's discount-exclusion list (Settings → POS Line-Item Discounts) — otherwise the button explains why and no discount is offered.

A discount up to the store's Maximum Discount % (Settings, the same limit that caps the Ticket Discount; 0 = unlimited) applies immediately. Going over that limit prompts for a manager PIN — an employee whose security group has pos_discount_override — entered on the spot; entering it records who authorized the discount right on the line. Every check (eligibility and the limit/override) is re-verified server-side at checkout too, so it can't be bypassed by tampering with the cart in the browser.

Holding an order

Use Hold (needs pos_hold) to park a cart without charging it or touching stock — useful when a customer steps away or a quote is being converted. Use Hold Recall to finish it later from the held-orders list, or delete the hold entirely to abandon it. Every card in that list — retail holds, pending rentals, rental balances due, and work orders alike — shows which employee placed it on hold and at which branch, alongside its type badge (RETAIL, RENTAL, RENTAL BALANCE DUE, or WORK ORDER), so a cashier picking up someone else's held ticket can see whose it was at a glance. A recalled ticket comes back with its discounts as they were held: each line's discount (including a manager override, still shown with who authorised it), and the ticket discount the same way it was given — a manual Ticket Discount keeps its amount, a promo code is re-applied (if the code no longer applies, the held discount amount is kept and the cashier is told), an automatic promotion is re-checked, and a customer's discount card or category discount comes back with the customer. A manual ticket discount also stays as entered when items are added or changed afterwards — automatic promotions don't replace it (clear it to let them apply).

Recording serial numbers — the SN item

For items that need a serial number captured on the sale, ring up the item as usual, then add the built-in SN item (search "SN" or tap its tile) right after it. You're asked for the serial number, and the ticket gets a line reading SN: serial directly under the item. Serials are optional — leave the prompt blank (or cancel) and no SN line is added; a sale or quote is never refused for a missing serial. SN is a $0, untaxed service with no stock, so it never changes the total or inventory — it only carries the serial. Each SN line holds exactly one serial (its quantity can't be raised), so for several units add SN once per unit. On the receipt, the letter-size invoice, emailed and PDF receipts and quotes, the serial prints as a plain Serial: serial note under the item — not as a $0.00 line — and it survives Hold/Recall and conversion to or from a quote. A serial that's actually an item number or barcode in the catalog (e.g. scanning the product's own code at the prompt) is refused with "… is the item number for …, not a serial number" — enter the serial printed on the product, or leave it blank to skip. On a quotation, pick SN as a line's product and a Serial # box appears beside it; an SN line left blank is simply dropped when the quote is saved.

Converting the current ticket to a Quote or Layaway

Create Quote and Create Layaway on the grid open the normal quotation/layaway tools (see Quotations), pre-filled from whatever's currently on the ticket — the attached customer, branch, and line items — so you don't have to re-enter them. Both forms stay fully editable before you save, so nothing is locked in by the prefill.

  • Quote: any item with variations (size/color/etc.) is added as a described custom line rather than a catalog line, since quotes don't track which specific variant was intended — the variant name is written into the description so it isn't lost.
  • Layaway: only items that are actually layaway-eligible can go on a layaway plan (see Inventory's services/layaway product settings) — any ticket item that isn't gets left out, with a warning naming how many were skipped, so you can add them to the layaway manually if needed. Creating the layaway clears the POS ticket, since the deposit is collected as part of that flow (needs layaway_create); the resulting plan then follows the normal layaway lifecycle in Layaway — collect installments, pick up on completion — with no difference from one created directly there.

Quote Recall on the grid opens the same quotation lookup tools without leaving the register screen.

Payment methods

Open Payment (needs pos_payment) on the button grid to choose a tender — Cash, Credit Card, Debit Card, Check, Gift Card, and Direct Deposit are all settled immediately. Pay On Acct (needs pos_pay_on_account) charges straight to a customer's credit account, which requires the account to be in good standing — a blocked account (overdue past its payment terms) is rejected at checkout with a clear error rather than silently failing.

If Alternate Currencies are configured (Settings), the Cash tender gets a Payment Currency picker — pick any of the store's base or alternate currencies and enter the amount actually handed over in that currency; it converts to the ticket's base currency automatically. Once there's change due, a separate Give Change In picker appears, independent of what currency was tendered — a customer can pay in USD and still get change back in the base currency, or vice versa, whatever the drawer actually has on hand. It defaults to matching the payment currency the first time it appears, but can be switched freely. The receipt and printed invoice always show change in whichever currency was chosen there; the transaction's own recorded amounts (for accounting/drawer reconciliation) stay in the base currency regardless.

Split Tender lets one sale be paid across two or more of the immediate-settlement methods above — for example part Cash and part Credit Card. Add a row per method, enter the exact amount each should cover, and add a reference/approval number where that method normally needs one (Credit Card, Debit Card, Gift Card, Direct Deposit); the rows must add up to the ticket total exactly before Confirm Payment is enabled. Pay On Acct can't be one of the split legs — a charge-account sale stays its own single-method tender. Since each leg is settled for its exact amount, there's no change-back on a split sale's cash leg the way there is on a plain Cash sale — if change is owed, apply a smaller amount to Cash and hand back the difference yourself. If Alternate Currencies are configured (see Settings), a Cash row gets its own currency picker just like the plain Cash tender does — enter the amount actually handed over in that currency and it converts to the ticket's base currency automatically. The receipt, transaction detail, and drawer reconciliation all show a split sale's tender legs itemized rather than as one payment method.

Tax exemption

Tax is normally waived automatically for a customer whose profile has the tax-exempt flag set. For a one-off exemption that shouldn't require editing that customer's record — a walk-in government or non-profit purchase, for instance — use Tax Exempt (needs pos_tax_exempt) on the button grid to mark just the current ticket, optionally recording an exemption number. This overrides the customer's saved flag for this sale only; the ticket totals update immediately, and the transaction/receipt are labeled tax-exempt the same way a profile-driven exemption would be.

Available credit (credit notes)

When a customer has an outstanding credit note — issued from a return (see Transactions & Returns) or a rental deposit (see Warehouse Management) — attaching them to a ticket shows an Available Credit bar with the balance the store owes them. Apply uses as much of it as the current ticket total needs (capped at whichever is smaller), reducing what's still owed by exactly the amount applied once the sale completes. If the applied credit brings the ticket to $0, opening Payment skips tender selection entirely and offers a single Complete Sale button instead — no cash/card/other tender needs to be picked, and the transaction is recorded as paid by Available Credit rather than as a $0 cash sale.

Cash back

If the attached customer carries an active Cash Back Card and has enough loyalty points accrued to clear its rules, a Cash Back bar appears the same way the Available Credit bar does. Apply uses as much of the available cash back as the ticket needs, and it stacks with Available Credit if both are active on the same customer — the ticket total is reduced by both. If the two together bring the ticket to $0, Payment again skips tender selection and offers a single Complete Sale button. Redeeming deducts the corresponding loyalty points proportionally (not the whole accrued chunk if only part of it was applied) and resets that card's redemption cooldown, if it has one.

Voids, cancels, and refunds

To take an item off a sale in progress, use the × on its row. Cancel Ticket (needs pos_cancel_ticket) clears the whole sale in progress. Refunding or returning items on an already-completed sale is handled from Transactions — the Returns button on the grid jumps straight there — and needs transactions_returns/pos_refunds.

Receipts and labels

Every receipt shows who created the ticket (whoever started it — e.g. the salesperson who held it, or the quote's original author) and the cashier who took payment; for an ordinary sale both are the same person. After checkout you can print a standard receipt, or re-open the last completed sale's receipt with Reprint Ticket (needs pos_reprint_ticket, scoped to the current register session). For shippable orders, print a pick list / shipping label from the Shipping screen — see Warehouse Management.

Cash Drawers drawers

Tracking physical cash in and out of the register across a shift.

  • Sessions — open a drawer at the start of a shift with a starting float, close it at the end. Only staff with drawers_open/drawers_close can do so, and reconciling a closed session (counting the actual cash against what the system expects) needs drawers_manage.
  • Reconciliation runs in three steps. 1. Return the float — count out the opening float and hand it back first; it's entered on its own line with its own variance, kept separate from the takings. 2. Count the cash takings — expected cash is cash sales and cash account payments, less any petty cash paid out. Count by denomination: the base currency (JMD) and every alternate currency set up in Settings (e.g. USD) each get their own note/coin table; foreign notes are multiplied by the Settings rate (Settings → Alternate Currencies asks what 1 unit is worth in JMD — e.g. 1 USD = 155 JMD — so US$3 counts as $465.00), and Apply total to Cash counted puts the combined figure in the Cash box. 3. Other tenders — card, check, gift card, charge account and direct deposit totals. The rate used is saved with the count, so later reports keep that day's rate. Drawers reconciled before the float step show the float as "counted with cash".
  • Available Credit on the drawer report is sales paid from customers' store credit (credit notes from returns, rental deposit credits) — no money changes hands, so it's listed for the sales total but never counted.
  • Petty Cash — a cashier with pos_petty_cash (Security Groups → Petty Cash, a separate tick not included with POS access; it starts on the Cashier and Senior Cashier groups) uses the POS Petty Cash button to pay cash out of their open drawer against a numbered petty cash request slip: voucher #, amount, paid to and reason. A voucher number can only be paid out once, and a payout can't exceed the cash in the drawer. Payouts are listed on the drawer report and come off expected cash; someone with drawers_manage can void one entered in error (with a reason) until the drawer is reconciled.
  • Denomination order — Settings → Currency Denominations: pick a currency, then drag a row by its ⠿ handle (mouse or touch) or use ↑ ↓ to put notes and coins in counting order. It saves as you go, and the drawer count and reports list them in that order — a newly added note goes to the bottom until you move it.
  • Petty cash on the Reconcile screen — the payouts from that drawer are listed (voucher, paid to, reason, amount) above the cash count, with the total taken off expected cash.
  • Drawer report — the float, takings by tender, petty cash, customer account payments and the cash count by denomination (per currency, with each foreign total's base value). 🖨 Print it, or ✉ Email PDF to send it as a PDF attachment. You can also email it straight from the Reconcile screen — tick Email the reconciliation report (PDF) when saved — or with ✉ Email on a reconciled drawer in the Sessions list. Set the usual recipient(s) in Settings → Currency Denominations → Email drawer reconciliation reports to (several separated by commas); the Reconcile screen then ticks Email for you.
  • Manage Drawers tab — set up which physical registers/drawers exist per branch, independent of who's using them on a given day.

Transactions & Returns transactions

The full history of completed (and held) sales, plus the returns workflow.

  • Search/filter by date, customer, status, branch, payment method, or transaction number.
  • The Type column reads Rental for a rental checkout/settlement, Work Order for a work order's assessment fee/deposit/final payment (linking straight to that agreement/work order), and Retail for everything else. Neither Rental nor Work Order transactions offer a Return button — they have no ordinary line items to return; use the Rentals or Work Orders screen's own return/pickup flow instead.
  • Returns (needs transactions_returns) — process a full or partial return against a completed sale; stock and any applicable fees are adjusted automatically. Picking how the customer is made whole is its own pair of permissions: Refund (cash or reversing the original payment method) needs transactions_refund, and Credit Note (adding the return value to the customer's store credit account instead — only offered when the sale has a customer attached) needs transactions_credit_note. A sale paid by credit card (fully, or as part of a split payment) can only be refunded within 90 days; after that the Refund option is greyed out and the return has to go out as a Credit Note or an exchange. A sale charged to the customer's account can never be refunded — it was never paid, so the Refund option is greyed out and the return goes out as a Credit Note, which takes it off what the customer owes (the server refuses a refund on one too). A security group missing one of these simply doesn't see that option in the return's Resolution picker — Replacement / Exchange isn't separately gated. A group with transactions_returns but neither resolution permission can still open a return, but is left with Replacement / Exchange as the only choice. Every return needs a reason and a manager's override: Reason for return is required (it prints on the return receipt as Reason and shows in the Returns list), and before Process Return a manager enters their PIN in Manager Override PIN — an employee whose security group has returns_override (Authorize Return Override, a standalone checkbox in Security Groups, so a full Transactions grant doesn't include it; it starts on Administrator and Manager). The server checks the PIN, a wrong one is cleared for re-entry, and the manager's name is recorded on the return: Authorized By on the return receipt (printed and emailed) and in the Returns list. On an exchange, the return credit stays on the new ticket as items are added or changed.
  • Finding a return later — returns aren't sales, so they don't appear in the sales list. Switch the first box on the Transactions screen from Sales to Returns to list returns for the chosen dates (search by return number, the original sale's number, or customer); View / Print opens the return receipt, which has Print and Email. A sale that has been returned shows a ↩ Returned badge in the sales list, and its detail window lists Returns on this sale — click a return number to open its receipt.
  • Exporting transaction history needs transactions_export.
  • Online Orders — sales that came in through the WooCommerce integration (or any API-key-authenticated channel) appear here tagged as online, with their own fulfillment status you can advance toward shipping. Its own screen (linked from the Sales hub) shows revenue/order-count/status stat cards above the list: with no filters applied they summarize today's online orders, and running a Search (date range, status, customer, or order number) recomputes them from just the filtered results instead, relabeled to make clear they're no longer "today only."
  • An employee flagged Salesperson only ever sees transactions they rang up or that are credited to them — a "My Sales Only" badge marks the list, and this is enforced server-side, not just hidden in the UI.
  • Salesperson column — who each sale counts for in sales targets: the salesperson who started the ticket or was picked on it, else whoever started the ticket, else the cashier.
  • Change Salesperson (needs sales_manager) — credit past sales to a different salesperson, e.g. sales made before the Salesperson field was in use. For one sale, open it and click Change Salesperson. For many, use the Any Cashier / Any Salesperson filters with the dates to find them (a sales manager's search shows up to 500), tick the sales (or the header box for all), then click Change Salesperson… above the list. Choose the salesperson and an optional reason; "None" puts a sale back to whoever started the ticket. Targets, the Salesperson Targets report and the salesperson's dashboard update straight away. Held and voided tickets can't be changed. Every change is logged and listed under Salesperson changes on the sale. Commission follows the sale: the previous salesperson's unpaid commission on it (pending or approved) is removed and worked out again on the new salesperson's plan as of the sale date, in the sale's month, as a new pending record (nothing if they have no plan). Commission already paid is never moved — those sales are listed in a warning so it can be adjusted by hand in Commissions. Rental commission stays with the rental agreement.
Held ordersA hold created by converting an accepted quotation is linked back to that quote — finishing the sale marks the quote "converted"; deleting the hold instead reverts the quote back to "accepted" so it isn't left stranded.

Quotations quotations

Formal price quotes for a customer, for either retail goods or a rental, that can later become a real sale.

Two kinds of quote

Retail quotes price from the current catalog and can mix real products with free-typed custom line items (for things not yet in your catalog) — the catalog product on a line is picked with a type-to-search box rather than scrolling a long dropdown, the same picker style as Work Orders' parts selection. Its Valid Until date defaults to 30 days out on a new quote — editable before saving, and left alone entirely on an existing one. Rental quotes are priced as a non-binding estimate using the item's daily/weekly/monthly rate over the requested date range, and can include delivery, pickup, and operator add-ons — they use a Due Date (the rental period end) instead of a Valid Until date. The Customer field (on quotations and Special Projects alike) is a type-to-search box — type part of a name, customer number, phone, or email and pick from the matches (the first 50 are shown — keep typing to narrow a common name down); the same searchable customer picker is used on New Work Order, Enable Credit Account, CRM opportunities/activities, and New Shipment. Every customer picker — these, plus New Rental, New Layaway, and POS Cust Lookup — also offers + Add New Customer (needs customers_add) at the top of its list: the customer form opens over the form you were filling in, pre-filled from what you typed, and once saved you're returned to that form with the new customer already selected and everything else you'd entered untouched. Switching Quote Type to Rental also narrows the Customer picker to customers already flagged Rental Customer on their profile (see Customers) — the same eligibility a customer needs before a rental agreement can be created directly. Rental items use the same type-to-search picker as the Retail item line, showing each match's current availability at the selected branch.

Valid Until on retail quotes and Special Projects has a 30 / 60 / 90 days dropdown beside the date picker, counted from the quote's own date (today, for a new quote). Picking one fills in the date; you can still pick any date directly, which shows as "Custom date".

C items (comments). + C Item on a retail quote or Special Project adds a comment line — free text such as "Price includes installation" or "Colour to be confirmed" that prints on the quote exactly as typed (line breaks kept). It has no quantity, price or tax, isn't a Q item, and never goes to Purchasing. When the quote is loaded into the POS or converted to an invoice, the comment comes with it and prints on the receipt and invoice. The same C Item button is on the POS button grid: select a ticket line first and the comment goes in right under it (otherwise at the end); double-click a comment line, or select it and press Item Zoom, to edit it, and × removes it. Comment lines are skipped by returns, and a quote or ticket needs at least one real item.

Delivery lines. + Delivery on a retail quote or Special Project adds a delivery charge as a line item: type a description (it starts as "Delivery" — e.g. change it to "Delivery to Ocho Rios") and the price before tax. Tax is added on top at the store's tax rate from Settings, shown beside the price (the form's Total is before tax, like the other lines; the saved quote shows the tax). It's always quantity 1 — add another delivery line for a second drop. It isn't a Q item and never goes to Purchasing. It carries through to the POS ticket and the invoice like any other line, taxed the same way (not taxed for a tax-exempt sale). Rental quotes keep their own Delivery / Pickup boxes instead. The same Delivery button is on the POS button grid: it asks for the description and the price before tax and adds the line to the ticket (quantity 1, taxed at the store rate); double-click the line, or select it and press Item Zoom, to change it, and × removes it. It's kept through Hold / Recall and Create Quote.

Special Orders. A retail quote or Special Project that includes a Q item — something not in the catalog that Purchasing has to order (on a retail quote, any custom line; on a Special Project, a line added with + Q Item) — is a Special Order. It carries a purple SPECIAL ORDER badge in the Quotations and Special Projects lists and on the quote itself, and each list has a Special orders only filter. Rental quotes are never special orders. To see them all in one place, open Sales → Special Orders: it lists every special order (retail and Special Project together), showing open ones by default, and can be narrowed by type, status, quote number or customer. Staff without Special Projects access only see the retail ones there.

Email as PDF. When emailing a quotation (retail, rental or Special Project), a sale's Retail Tax Invoice, a Rental Agreement (the checkout invoice), a Rental Tax Invoice (the Full Summary), a Rental Settlement (the sale settling a return), a Work Order Tax Invoice, an approved Purchase Order, or a Goods Received Note, the email dialog has an Attach as PDF option. Ticked, the customer gets a short cover email with the document attached as a letter-size PDF (same contents, plus any signatures on file); unticked, the document is in the email body as before. The dialog remembers your last choice on that computer.

Status flow

  1. Draft — still being built; can be freely edited.
  2. Sent — shared with the customer.
  3. Accepted — the customer has committed. For retail quotes, this is the moment anything not currently in stock at the quote's branch is automatically handled: stock available at another branch triggers an automatic branch transfer, and anything no branch can cover (or a custom item never in the catalog) is bundled into a single purchase request for Purchasing to action.
  4. Converted — the quote becomes real: a retail quote becomes a held order a cashier finalizes at the POS; a rental quote becomes a rental agreement (after re-checking stock and the customer's rental eligibility, since time may have passed since the quote was drafted).

A quote can also be declined instead of accepted. Needs quotations_create to build one, quotations_approve to move it through status changes, and quotations_convert to finalize it into a sale or agreement.

Per-line discount

Each retail line — a real product or a free-typed custom item — has its own optional Discount field, separate from the whole-quote Discount ($) field. It's a record of how much was knocked off that specific line, shown next to the item wherever the line shows up; it doesn't itself change the price you type in — enter the already-discounted price there. If the line isn't in stock and gets bundled into a Purchase Request (or later a Purchase Order) at Accept, its discount comes along too, shown as a small tag on that line — so whoever approves the PR or PO can see the item is backing a discounted sale before signing off.

Attachments

Any quote — retail, rental, or Special Project — has an Attachments section at the bottom of its detail view. + Attach Document uploads a file (PDF, Word, Excel, image or text, up to 20MB) tagged as a Customer PO, Supplier Quotation, Drawing / Specification, or Other; each one lists its type, size, upload date and who uploaded it, with Download and remove buttons. Attachments can be added at any stage, including after the quote is converted — e.g. a customer PO that arrives after acceptance. Attachments (like PO attachments) are only reachable while signed in — opening a download link in a browser with no active session shows the login screen first, and the file downloads once you sign in.

Reassigning a quote

Someone with the sales_manager permission can hand an open quote (not yet converted or declined) to a different salesperson using the Reassign button on the quote's detail view. This is for handing off the follow-through work — the new assignee becomes who the quote shows as "Assigned to." Commission on the eventual sale still goes to whoever the quote was originally assigned to (shown as "Sale credited to" once a quote has been reassigned) — reassigning it again keeps tracing credit back to that same original salesperson, not whoever it was most recently passed from.

The Quotations list marks any reassigned quote with a small "↪ reassigned" tag next to its current assignee, and a Reassigned only checkbox (alongside the Status filter) narrows the list down to just those — the quickest way to see everything currently out with someone other than the salesperson who'll be credited for it.

An employee flagged Salesperson (see Employees) sees two tabs at the top of Quotations: My Quotes (the default — everything currently assigned to them, including anything handed to them via Reassign) and All Quotes. This is a convenience filter only — it doesn't restrict which quotes they can open, unlike the hard scoping applied to Transactions, CRM, and Commissions.

The list can also be searched directly: a Quote # box, a Customer name box, and a Date picker (matches the quote's created date) all narrow the list as you type/pick, alongside the Status filter. A My Quotes Only checkbox is available to everyone — not just employees flagged Salesperson — for quickly narrowing the list down to whatever's assigned to whoever is currently logged in.

Special Projects special_projects

Custom quotes/invoices for one-off jobs — tree removal, a special install, anything that isn't a normal retail sale — where line items are typed in freely (labour, equipment rental, tools) with an optional catalog item mixed in, usually priced above cost. Its own button on the Sales hub, its own permissions, and its own screen — separate from Quotations even though it's built on the same underlying quote.

Who can use it

Deliberately restricted to a handful of people, independent of ordinary Quotations access: special_projects to build, submit, and convert a project; special_projects_approve to approve or reject one. Having quotations alone does not grant either — a Special Project is invisible in both the Special Projects screen and the API to anyone without one of these two permissions, even in an unfiltered list. The two are genuinely independent permissions, not a module-and-sub-permission pair — checking special_projects in Security Groups does not also grant approval rights, so the same person doesn't have to both set a project's pricing and sign off on it.

Line items and markup

Each line is either a real catalog product or a free-typed custom item (same "pick a product OR type a description" builder Quotations uses) — but unlike a regular quote's custom line, a Special Project's + Custom Item lines never generate a Purchasing "Q" flag or a Purchase Request; they're priced references and labour/service charges, not things the store needs to go buy. For something that does need ordering, use + Q Item instead: it's entered the same way (description, qty, cost, price) but carries the orange Q badge, and when the project is accepted (or converted straight to an invoice) every Q item goes into one auto-created Purchase Request for the project — exactly like a regular quote's Q items. Every line also has an optional Cost field alongside Price (pre-filled from the catalog for a real product), with a live markup/margin percentage shown next to it — purely informational, it never changes what gets charged.

Rental items on a Special Project. + Rental Item adds a rental item for a set period: pick the item, the quantity, and the start and end dates (the end date is when it's due back). The price fills in from the item's rental rates over that period (the same billing rules as a normal rental) and can be changed like any other project price; under the line you'll see the length, the catalog rate, and how many are free at the project's branch right now. Mandatory accessories are added automatically as "included" lines at no charge. The rental charge is a line on the project invoice, taxed at the item's rate, and no rental deposit is taken.

  • Hold: approving the project holds the item — it creates a held rental agreement (shown in Rentals → Held with an SP badge) and the item is no longer available to anyone else. Approval is refused if there aren't enough free at the branch, if the project has no customer or branch, or if the customer isn't an approved rental customer or has a returned rental still unpaid. Sending the project back to Draft (or rejecting or declining it) releases the hold; approving again holds it again.
  • Payment: the held rental isn't paid on its own — there's no Process Payment on it, and it doesn't appear in POS Hold Recall. It's paid when the cashier collects the project invoice; that moves it to Awaiting Issue.
  • Check-out and check-in: issue and return it from the Rentals screen as usual (security sign-off, condition, signatures). The rental clock starts when it's issued.
  • On the project: under each rental line the project view shows its rental agreement and status, the check-out and check-in times and who signed, the condition out and in, and any charges raised at check-in.
  • Charges at check-in: as with any rental, the charge is recalculated for the actual time out. A late return or damage leaves a balance owed, collected from Rentals → Returned – Not Paid (the customer can't start another rental until it's paid); an early return leaves a refund due, handled in Rental Deposits.
  • Returns: the project invoice's other lines can be returned from Transactions as normal; its rental lines can't — they're checked back in from Rentals.

Approval, before anything else

  1. Draft — build the project, add items, adjust pricing. Freely editable.
  2. Submit for Approval — locks the draft and hands it to whoever holds special_projects_approve.
  3. Approved (or Rejected, back to Draft with a required reason, shown as a banner on the project until it's resubmitted) — a supervisor's pricing sign-off, weighing things like current workload and how difficult the job looks. There's no separate sign-off step beyond this — approving is the decision. The approver's signature is applied (see Approval signatures) and shows on the project and on its printed, emailed and PDF quotation.
  4. Sent → Accepted → Converted — once approved, a Special Project behaves exactly like a retail quote: send it to the customer, record their acceptance, then Convert sends it to the POS as a held order for a cashier to collect payment on, the same "Recall Held Order" pattern every other invoice in this app uses.

A Special Project can only be edited while still a Draft — editing after submission would silently invalidate a sign-off that was granted on different numbers. Sending, accepting, or converting is blocked with a clear error until it's Approved.

Attachments

Special Projects share the quote Attachments section (see Quotations) — useful for drawings, site specs, supplier quotes backing the costing, and the customer's PO. Anyone who can see the project (special_projects or special_projects_approve) can view and download them; adding or removing one needs special_projects, so an approver can review the supporting documents without changing them. Unlike the line items, attachments aren't locked once the project leaves Draft.

Commission

No separate commission setup needed — a converted Special Project becomes a real completed sale exactly like a converted retail quote, so it's picked up by whatever commission plan already applies to sales (see Commissions), crediting whoever the project is assigned to.

Margin Report

The 📊 Margin Report button opens a date-range/branch/status-filtered breakdown of every Special Project's revenue, cost, and margin — cost comes from each line's Cost field (see Line items and markup above). Two tables: By Project (each project's own margin, with a ⚠ next to any project that has a line with no cost entered — that project's margin is understated, treated as $0 cost on that line) and By Salesperson (the same figures rolled up by whoever the project is credited to, same as commission crediting). Gated by the same special_projects / special_projects_approve permissions as the rest of this screen — deliberately not folded into the main Reports & Analytics screen, since that one only needs the much more broadly-held reports permission and this report exposes cost/margin data on individually-negotiated deals.

Layaway layaway

Reserve merchandise for a customer against a schedule of installment payments.

  • Only products explicitly marked layaway-eligible can go on a plan — picked from a searchable box (type a few letters of the name or SKU) rather than scrolling the full list.
  • Stock is reserved immediately when the plan is created, not when it's paid off — so it won't get sold to someone else in the meantime.
  • A minimum deposit (a store-wide percentage) is required up front; the customer can choose weekly, biweekly, or monthly installments after that.
  • Every deposit and installment is recorded as a real transaction, so it shows up in normal cash-drawer and sales reporting.
  • Paying off the full balance auto-completes the plan and awards loyalty points.
  • Cancelling a plan restores the reserved stock and refunds whatever the customer already paid, minus a configurable cancellation fee (percentage or flat) that's forfeited.

Needs layaway_create to start a plan, layaway_payments to record installments, layaway_cancel to cancel one.

Rentals rentals

Rent out equipment instead of selling it, with its own catalog, compliance requirements, and checkout/return workflow.

The top-right of the Rentals screen has three shortcuts: Rental Quote (needs quotations) opens a new quotation with Quote Type already set to Rental — see Quotations; Rental Dispatch (rentals_issue) jumps to the dispatch queue below; and + New Rental (rentals_checkout) starts a rental directly instead of quoting it first.

Rental Items

Managed separately from retail Inventory. Each item has a daily rate, a classification (Tool or Equipment), a model number and size, and can have mandatory or optional accessories attached (needs rentals_manage_items).

Tool pricing tiersTools get automatic volume discounts off the daily rate instead of a manually-set weekly/monthly catalog price: renting 7-28 days bills the whole rental at 85% of the daily rate per day, and renting more than 28 days bills the whole rental at 75% of that (i.e. 63.75% of the original daily rate) per day. The Weekly/Monthly Rate fields on the item form are only used for Equipment items, which still bill in fixed week/month blocks at their own configured rates plus an hourly rate for partial days — a leftover partial day of 8 hours or less bills hourly, but once it exceeds 8 hours the whole day rolls over and bills at the full daily rate instead (a full working day's worth of use, effectively). Tools have no such rollover — a partial day always prorates continuously by the hour, no matter how long it runs.
Choosing the rateNew Rental and rental quotes have a Rate picker under the due date: Automatic (the default — the rate the rental length earns, as above), Daily, Weekly or Monthly. A chosen rate can only hold the rental at or below the tier its length earns, never lift it: Weekly needs a rental of at least 7 days, and Monthly needs more than 28 days (28 or more for Equipment), so tiers the due date doesn't reach are greyed out and the server refuses them too. Equipment also needs its Weekly/Monthly Rate set on the item for those choices. Daily bills every day at the full daily rate however long it runs; Weekly never drops to the monthly rate. If a rental comes back early, it's billed at the rate its actual length earns (a Monthly rental returned at day 10 bills at the weekly rate). The chosen rate carries over when a quote is converted, and shows as Rate on the agreement and quote. Special Project rental lines have the same picker on each line (each line has its own period); the chosen rate sets the line's catalog price and is carried onto the rental that's held when the project is approved, so a late or early return is settled at that rate too.

Bulk Tax Rate Change (needs rentals_manage_items) overwrites the tax rate on active rental items — pick All Branches to update every rental item regardless of which branch it's assigned to, or pick one branch to only touch the items currently assigned there. See how many items that affects before committing, enter the new rate, and confirm — like the Inventory screen's own Bulk Tax Rate Change (below), this always overwrites, with no gap-only mode. This tool only ever touches rental items (is_rental), while Inventory's version isn't type-filtered — running that one at a branch also overwrites the rate on any rental items assigned there, alongside the retail and non-inventory items it's meant for. Use this rental-specific tool when you only want to touch rentals.

Selling a rental item outright

Checking "Also allow selling or quoting this item outright, like a retail item" on the item form (with a required Selling Price) makes it show up in POS's product grid and a Retail Quotation's item picker too — tagged RENTAL ITEM / [Rental] so staff can tell it apart from ordinary stock — in addition to still being rentable. It's a genuine outright sale: the unit sold is deducted from stock for good, the same as any retail item, and doesn't come back the way a returned rental does. Only units not currently out on an active rental are offered for sale — the item's stock figure is the total number owned, so whatever's checked out to a rental customer is excluded from what POS/Quotations will sell. The For Sale · [price] tag on the Rental Items list shows which items this is turned on for.

Moving a rental item between locations

A rental item lives at a single branch at a time (its Branch / Location on the item form) — only staff at that branch, or with multi-branch access, can see and rent it. There are two ways to change that:

  • Edit the item directly — changing the Branch / Location dropdown moves its stock to the new branch instantly, with no transfer record. Fine for an item that's never been rented, but blocked (with an error naming how many units and telling you to use Branch Transfers instead) whenever units of it are still out on an active rental from its current branch — reassigning it out from under an active loan would leave that rental's return pointing at a branch the item no longer shows as belonging to.
  • Transfer (needs transfers) — the Transfer button on the item's row in Rental Items jumps into Branch Transfers with the item and its current branch pre-filled, going through the same pickup/receive audit trail retail stock uses. Only units actually free right now can be moved — a rental item's stock figure is the total number owned, so whatever's currently checked out to a customer from that branch is subtracted from what's offered for transfer, both in the item picker and if attempted anyway.

Customer eligibility

A customer can't start a new rental until their profile has an ID (type, number, and a scanned copy), an uploaded proof-of-address document, and three references on file — including a scanned photo ID for the main reference. This is enforced at both the direct rental-hold flow and at converting a rental quote into an agreement.

Rental Dispatch — the agreement lifecycle

  1. Create — pick items and a due date; availability is checked for that window. Picking a due date shows the equivalent day count right beside it, or you can work the other way and type a number of days into the field below it to have the due date filled in automatically (starting from now).
  2. Checkout (needs rentals_checkout) — finalizes pricing and takes the deposit. The Refundable Deposit equals the rental fee plus tax on it (the Sales Total) — a customer pays the fee and tax as the actual sale, then that same amount again as a fully-refundable hold, so the register total is double the Sales Total plus any delivery/pickup/operator fees. The printed/emailed Rental Agreement (🖨️ Rental Agreement / ✉️ Email Agreement on the agreement) breaks this out as Sub-Total Amount / Sales Tax / Sales Total / Rental Deposit / Grand Total, and names the employee who processed the checkout as Created By. No deposit is charged when an Operator is required — our own staff runs the equipment rather than the customer, so there's nothing a deposit needs to protect against; the register total in that case is just the Sales Total plus service fees, not doubled. If billed to the customer's charge account (Charge Account (Credit) payment method), a customer PO number and a copy of the PO document are required before the checkout can complete — the PO is what ties the account charge back to the customer's own paperwork. Split Tender is also available at checkout to spread the total across two or more methods (e.g. part cash, part card) — Charge Account and Gift Card can't be split legs, and since the refund policy below keys off a single original method, a split-paid deposit's refund method is left to staff to choose at return.
  3. Issue (needs rentals_issue) — records the physical handoff to the customer; this can happen on a different date than checkout, and can include delivery (with driver and cost) or a customer pickup, plus an optional equipment operator. Required to complete this step: a security employee verifying the item leaving and signing to confirm it, plus the customer's own signature (drawn on-screen) confirming receipt — two separate signatures, each viewable individually from the agreement's detail view. A delivery agreement can instead go through this as three separate self-service steps spread across security, driver, and customer — see the callout below — with a rentals_issue holder able to complete it the traditional single-step way at any point, picking up whichever checkpoints are already on file.
  4. Return (needs rentals_returns) — processes the item coming back, assessing any late fees or damage fees against the deposit. Required to complete this step: a security employee signing the item back in — always. A driver select, confirming who collected the item, only appears when the agreement has pickup as part of it; a walk-in return with no pickup shows no driver field at all. If the driver already confirmed the pickup in the field (see below), a green banner replaces the signature pad — no redundant signature is collected here. A rental can't be returned while it's paused (see below) — resume it first.

An agreement can also be cancelled with a reason on file instead of following through to return.

Assigning & confirming a pickup driverUnlike delivery (claimed by a driver, not pre-assigned — see below), a pickup driver can be assigned ahead of the actual pickup — click Assign next to "Not yet assigned" in the Pickup column (Rental Dispatch's Deliveries tab, the Rentals list, or the agreement detail view; needs rentals_issue). That driver then sees the job under Pickups Needed on their own My Deliveries dashboard and, if their security group has rentals_confirm_pickup, taps Confirm Pickup once they've actually collected the item — the customer enters their name and signs on the driver's device right there, no cashier involved. This only records that the item was physically collected; it doesn't touch billing. Process Return still finalizes the financial settlement afterward (still gated on rentals_returns, independently of confirm-pickup access), whenever it's convenient for a cashier to do it — it just skips asking for another driver signature since the field confirmation already has one.
Confirm-pickup access is its own permissionrentals_confirm_pickup is separate from rentals_returns on purpose — a security group can be set up so a driver sees and confirms their assigned pickups (the signature capture) without ever being able to open Process Return. Without this permission, the job still shows on the driver's My Deliveries dashboard (so they know what's expected of them) but the Confirm Pickup button itself is hidden, and the backend rejects the action if called directly. Being flagged Driver on an employee's profile only controls whether they land on My Deliveries and which jobs are assigned to them — it does not by itself grant any action; that's the security group's job.
Delivery: security signs, then a driver claims and signs twiceA delivery agreement's Issue step splits into three self-service checkpoints instead of one combined office step, since the customer isn't at the counter to sign. First, any employee flagged Security whose security group has rentals_security_signoff signs off from their own My Verifications dashboard (Sign Off button under Awaiting Security Check) — no employee picker, they're signing for themselves. The job then appears to every driver under Deliveries Ready to Claim on My Deliveries (first-come-first-served — there's no dispatcher pre-assignment for delivery the way there is for pickup); a driver whose security group has rentals_confirm_delivery taps Accept & Sign for Delivery and signs to take custody, which moves the job to Out for Delivery — Awaiting Customer Signature but does not start the rental clock. Once at the delivery address, that same driver taps Get Customer Signature, has the customer enter their name and sign, and that's what actually completes the issue and starts the clock — same as the traditional in-office flow's customer signature always has. A rentals_issue holder can still open the traditional Issue modal on a delivery agreement at any point and finish it the old combined way — whichever checkpoints already have a signature show as a read-only confirmation banner instead of being asked for again.
My Deliveries shows drivers only what they needThe driver dashboard's delivery queue is deliberately not the same data every other rental screen uses — it comes from a separate, restricted endpoint that never includes deposits, fees, totals, or any other transactional figure, only the agreement number, the item(s), the delivery address, and the customer's name and phone. A driver's security group never needs the broad rentals permission just for this dashboard to work.
Security employeesOnly employees flagged Security on their profile (Employees → edit, alongside the existing Driver/Operator checkboxes) appear in the Issue/Return security dropdowns. Since these checkpoints are required, at least one employee needs that flag set before any rental can be issued or returned — and at both Issue and Return, that employee has to draw their own signature on-screen (not just pick their name) to confirm. The rentals list shows a "✓ Issue" / "✓ Return" badge once each side's checkpoints — including the guard's signature — are on file; a dash means either that stage hasn't happened yet or (for rentals issued/returned before this existed) there's no record.
A balance owed at return goes to the register, not the guardThe item itself comes back into stock the moment security signs it in, regardless of any money owed — but if the settlement (damage fees plus any duration adjustment, less the deposit) comes out positive on a non-credit rental, the return completes with no transaction charged yet rather than assuming whoever processed the return collected it in cash. The agreement sits fully Returned and shows up in the POS Hold Recall list labeled RENTAL BALANCE DUE with the amount owed, the same "pay at the register" pattern Work Orders use for their final balance. A cashier picks it up from there, sees the full breakdown (damage fees, duration adjustment, tax, less the deposit), and takes payment — Split Tender is available here too (any method except Charge Account) — and that's what actually creates the settlement transaction, tied to their own drawer session, with a receipt (including the letter-size invoice) shown immediately after. A return that nets a refund instead (deposit exceeds what's owed) still settles immediately at the point of return — there's no cash to collect from the customer, so there's nothing to hold.

Pausing a rental

Returns due on a closed day. A branch can be marked Closed on Sundays & public holidays (Branch Management → edit the branch). For rentals out of that branch, one due back on a Sunday or on a holiday listed in Settings → Rental Returns — Closed Days can be returned by 8:00 AM on the next open day with no late charge — the time after it was due isn't billed. If the next day is also closed (a Sunday followed by a Monday holiday), the grace runs to the following open day. Returned after that time, the late charge covers the whole time since it was due, as normal. While a rental is inside its grace period it isn't listed as overdue, and the Rentals list, the agreement and the Process Return window all show when it may come back by. The same Settings card sets the return-by time (8:00 AM by default), the store's time zone (that time is store time), and the holiday list — one per line, date first, e.g. 2026-12-25 Christmas Day.

An issued (active) rental can be paused for Replacement, Repair, Servicing, Maintenance or Relocation — for when the item has to come back to the shop or be moved mid-rental without that counting as a return. Pausing stops the billing clock: the paused time is excluded from the rental fee charged at return, and the due date is automatically pushed out by however long the pause lasted (rounded up to the nearest whole day) once it's resumed.

Operator and negotiated stops — scheduling. Two more pause reasons cover agreed downtime: Operator stop (the operator isn't working, e.g. over a weekend) and Negotiated stop (a stop agreed with the customer). These two can be booked ahead: in Pause / Schedule Stop, set Stop to At a date and time, and Resume to Automatically at a later one (or leave it to resume manually). Any pause, of any reason, can also be set to resume automatically — when pausing, or afterwards with Set Resume Time on the paused agreement. A booked stop starts and ends on its own at exactly the times set, so the billing clock and the due-date extension are worked out from those times; it needs the same manager PIN as pausing now. Booked stops are listed on the agreement under Scheduled stops (with Cancel), and the Rentals list shows Stop scheduled … or resumes … under the due date. Stops can't overlap each other; to book one while the rental is already paused, give the current pause a resume time first (Schedule Stop on a paused rental). A booked stop is cancelled automatically if the rental is returned or cancelled before it starts.

Recurring stops. For an operator or negotiated stop, set Repeat to Daily (e.g. every night 5:00 PM to 7:00 AM — a resume time earlier than the stop time means the next morning) Weekly (e.g. every Saturday 12:00 PM to Monday 7:00 AM), or Specific days — tick the days a stop starts on (Monday to Friday is ticked to begin with) and give one stop and resume time, e.g. Mon–Fri nights 5:00 PM to 7:00 AM, with a Starting date and an optional Until date (blank = until the rental is returned). Times are store time. The stops are booked two weeks ahead on a rolling basis and each one starts and ends on its own like any scheduled stop; if one is already under way when you set it up, it starts straight away. The agreement shows the pattern under Recurring stops with the next stop and how many have been taken; Stop Repeating cancels the ones not yet started (one already under way carries on until it resumes). A recurring stop gives way to anything else: if the rental is already paused when one comes due, or it would overlap a one-off scheduled stop, that occurrence is skipped. The due date moves out by the total time stopped across the series, rounded up to whole days — so nightly stops of 14 hours add a day about every second night, not a day each night.

Maintenance log. Pausing for Repair, Servicing or Maintenance asks which item(s) are going in (the main item is ticked; tick accessories too if they're going in). Each one gets a record under Rentals → In Maintenance, with the pause notes as its first log entry. That list shows every item currently in the shop — the agreement and customer, branch, how long it's been in, the latest log entry and the cost so far. Log / Update opens the item's full work log, where anyone working on it adds entries as they go: Issue found, Work done, Parts or Note, each with an optional cost, stamped with who and when. Mark Complete signs the work off when the item's ready; resuming the rental also completes any record still open. Tick Include completed maintenance to see past work, and the agreement itself lists its maintenance with a link to each log. Logged costs are a record only — they aren't charged to the customer.

Pause requires manager overrideAnyone who can see the agreement can open Pause Rental, but starting the pause requires a manager/admin PIN on the spot (needs rentals_pause) — same override pattern as voiding a POS cart line — plus a reason and notes explaining what's happening. Resuming doesn't need a PIN. While paused, the agreement shows a Paused status on the list (with the current pause's start time) and a full Pause History — reason, notes, start/stop times, who paused/authorized/resumed it, and the due-date extension — is kept on the agreement's detail view and on its printed invoice.

Missed pickups

Delivery and pickup addresses are always required. Whenever Delivery required or Pickup required is ticked — on + New Rental or on a rental quote — the Delivery Address / Pickup Address must be filled in, even when it's the customer's own address. Neither is ever filled in from the address on file, so staff have to ask the customer where it's going and where it's being collected from. same as delivery address next to the pickup address copies the delivery address just entered, for the common case where it's collected from the same place. The pickup address is what pickup drivers see on Rental Dispatch and My Deliveries. A rental quote saved before this rule that has delivery or pickup but no address can't be converted until the address is added to the quote; older rentals without a pickup address still show the delivery or customer address as before.

Changing the pickup after the fact. On the agreement (needs rentals_issue), Change Pickup edits the pickup address or fee, or Cancel Pickup removes it (a reason is required — e.g. the customer will bring it back themselves); on a rental without one, + Add Pickup adds it, with the address required as always. This works until the item is collected or returned. If the rental hasn't been paid yet, the fee is simply what the cashier charges at checkout. Once it's paid, the difference from the pickup fee charged at checkout is settled with the return: a pickup added (or a higher fee) is added to what's owed, and a pickup cancelled (or a lower fee) is credited — shown live in the window before you save, on the agreement until the return, and as its own Pickup Fee line on the settlement and the Rental Tax Invoice (Full Summary). Cancelling also clears the pickup driver and booked time. Every change is listed under Pickup Changes on the agreement with who made it, when, and why.

Scheduling deliveries and pickups. Rental Dispatch opens on the Schedule tab: every delivery and pickup booked for the chosen dates (Today, Tomorrow, Next 7 days, or your own range), grouped by day and listed by time, with the customer and phone, address, items, driver and status (Booked, Out for delivery, Done, or Late once the time has passed). Filter by driver to see one driver's run. Needs Scheduling at the top lists the open jobs nobody has booked yet — a delivery not yet out, or a pickup not yet collected — oldest due first. Schedule / Reschedule (needs rentals_issue; also on the agreement under Dispatch) sets the date, a start time and optional end time for the window, the driver, and notes for the driver (gate code, contact on site…); Unschedule clears it. Choosing a driver for a delivery reserves it for them — other drivers no longer see it to claim — and choosing one for a pickup assigns it the same way as Assign. You're warned (not stopped) if the driver already has an overlapping job, or if a pickup is booked after the rental is due back. Drivers see the booked time and notes on their My Deliveries jobs, in time order. A delivery can be booked until it goes out, a pickup until it's collected. The delivery and pickup can also be booked right away on + New Rental: tick Delivery required or Pickup required and fill in Schedule delivery / Schedule pickup (date, from/to time, driver, notes; use due date fills the pickup date with the rental's due date) — both are optional, and leaving the date blank books it later instead. The driver choice there is only shown to staff who can schedule from Rental Dispatch.

Rental Dispatch's Pickup Reminders tab highlights every active, pickup-required rental approaching its due date/time — not just the day, the actual hour, combining the due date with the time of day the rental was checked out at. Rows are bucketed and color-coded: amber for due within 24 hours, red for due within 8 hours. It's a live view, not a sent notification — nothing is emailed or logged just for appearing on this list.

If the due date/time passes and the item still hasn't come back, the system automatically pauses the agreement (reason Missed Pickup) — no manager PIN, since there's no one present to enter one for a system action. The agreement's detail view then shows a highlighted callout in place of the normal pause banner, with three actions:

  • Email Customer — sends a follow-up email (editable message, defaults to a standard "we couldn't complete your pickup" note) and logs that the attempt was made.
  • Log Call — for when staff called instead; just records the outcome, no email sent.
  • Record Customer Decision — once staff has actually reached the customer, this is where their decision gets documented: Continue (pick a new pickup date; the pause ends and the due date updates to it) or Stop (the pause ends with no extension — the agreement drops back into the normal Active/Overdue view, unblocked for Process Return whenever the item actually comes back). Either way an optional confirmation email can go out to the customer summarizing what was agreed.
The customer's decision is staff-attested, not self-serviceThere's no reply-parsing or click-a-link infrastructure in this app — recording "the customer confirmed continue/stop" means a staff member is vouching for a conversation that happened by phone or email, the same way every other rental checkpoint in this app (guard/customer signatures) is a human physically confirming something, not a customer-facing portal. The confirmation, whoever logged it, and when, are all kept on that specific missed-pickup pause record — visible in Pause History on the agreement.

Driver & Guard dashboards

An employee flagged Driver or Security (Employees → edit) lands on a purpose-built dashboard instead of the regular Dashboard when they log in — neither role needs sales totals or low-stock counts, just their own rental checkpoint queue.

  • My Deliveries (Driver) — Deliveries Needed (paid, awaiting issue, delivery required, not yet dispatched — a shared pool any driver can act on, since delivery has no pre-assignment step), Pickups Needed (only pickups a dispatcher has assigned to this driver and not yet confirmed, soonest due date first, each with a Confirm Pickup action), and My Recent Activity (deliveries/pickups this employee has personally completed).
  • My Verifications (Security) — Awaiting Security Check (paid, awaiting issue, no security sign-off yet), Expected Returns (all active rentals, soonest due date first, so a guard knows what to expect at the gate), and My Recent Verifications (issue/return sign-offs this employee has personally recorded).

Deliveries and security checks are still a shared pool anyone with the flag can act on — they're recorded at the moment Issue or Return actually happens, with no assignment ahead of time. Pickups are the one exception (see the callout above): a dispatcher assigns a specific driver first, so that driver's dashboard only shows their own jobs, not everyone's. Clicking View on a Guard's row opens the same agreement detail used everywhere else in Rentals; on My Deliveries, My Recent Activity rows show the delivery/pickup address instead and have no View link — a driver's security group is only ever meant to hold rentals_confirm_pickup/rentals_confirm_delivery, and those two never unlock the full agreement detail, the Rentals/Rental Dispatch/Rental Deposits/Rental Items screens, or their sidebar tiles, even though App's permission check is normally bidirectional (a sub-permission alone would otherwise imply the parent module). An employee flagged as both Driver and Security lands on My Deliveries by default but can still reach My Verifications from the sidebar.

Rental Deposits & Credit Notes

Tracks deposits collected and refunded per agreement, and lets staff issue a credit note when a partial or adjusted refund is warranted instead of a straight deposit return. Summary cards at the top break down every deposit currently in the system: how much is still Held on active/awaiting-issue rentals, how much has been refunded through each channel (Cash, Bank Transfer, Original Card), how much went out as Store Credit, how much was Applied to Account (charge-account rentals — automatic, no manual step), and how much is Awaiting Method — a refund is due but nobody has recorded how it was actually paid out yet. The list below is sorted by deposit status — Held first, then Partially Applied, Fully Applied and Fully Refunded (rentals with no deposit last), newest first within each — and can be filtered by agreement number or customer, by deposit status, and by refund/credit status (e.g. Refund Due to see what still needs paying out).

When a return leaves a refund due (the row itself needs rentals_returns to have been processed), each row can offer two separately-permissioned actions: Issue Credit Note (needs rentals_credit_note — puts the refund on the customer's account as store credit instead of paying it out), and Record Refund (needs rentals_deposit_refund) — logs which of Cash, Bank Transfer, or Refund to Original Card was actually used, plus an optional reference number (transfer ref, approval code) and notes. This is bookkeeping only: the money itself is expected to move outside the app (or already has) the moment the return posts a refund-type settlement — this step just records which channel it went through, for the summary cards above and for the policy check below. A security group can hold one of these without the other — e.g. a cashier who can record how a cash refund went out but can't independently issue store credit. Both actions immediately offer the Rental Tax Invoice (the Full Summary — titled RENTAL TAX INVOICE when printed or emailed) — one comprehensive, printable/emailable document (prefilled with the customer's address on file) covering the whole agreement: checkout date/time versus actual return, per-item daily/weekly/monthly rate with its original estimated fee next to the final fee and any damage fee, the checkout charges (fee, tax, deposit, total paid), the return settlement (damage/duration/tax adjustments against the deposit), and however the resulting balance or refund was disposed of — collected, issued as store credit, refunded by cash/bank/card, or applied automatically to a charge account. The same document (with all Issue and Return signatures on file, shown side by side in one row) is reachable any time afterward via the row's Receipt button, or from the agreement's own detail view as Full Summary.

The refund method dropdown is pre-selected per policy, not lockedBased on the deposit's original payment method, amount, and age, the recorded refund method defaults to: Cash/Debit deposits of J$10,000 or less → Cash; over J$10,000 → Bank Transfer. Credit card deposits refunded within 90 days of the original charge → back to the original card; past 90 days → Bank Transfer. Direct Deposit, Bank Transfer, and Cheque originals → Bank Transfer only. The policy note shown in the modal explains which rule applied — staff can still pick a different method for a real exception, it just won't carry that note.

Agreements returned before this tracking existed show Awaiting Method until someone goes back and records how that refund was actually handled — a one-time backfill pass, not something the app can infer on its own.

Work Orders work_orders

A ticketing system for service/repair intake — a customer drops off an item, staff open a Work Order (WO) against it, and it moves through assessment, deposit, and repair before the customer is notified and the item is picked up.

Lifecycle

  1. Intake (needs wo_intake) — pick the customer, optionally the branch, an item/tag label, an Assessment Fee, and a description of what's being serviced. A label prints automatically showing the WO number, ready to attach to the item. The description stays editable at any point before completion — changing it is logged in the status history, not silently overwritten.
  2. Assessment Fee — picked at intake from the Services catalog, searchable the same way parts are (see below), since different machine types typically carry different flat fees. Whichever one's picked is snapshotted onto the WO — price, name, and that service's own tax rate — so a later price/rate change or rename to that service doesn't retroactively change what an already-open WO shows or charges. This non-refundable fee (plus tax) is collected before the item is assessed; paying it moves status to Assessed.
  3. Estimate (needs wo_assess) — labor and consumables (oil, grease, fuel, etc.) are entered as estimate dollar amounts, along with an optional deposit amount. Tax on labor/consumables uses the company's default tax rate (Settings), snapshotted onto the WO the moment the estimate is saved — a later change to that default doesn't retroactively change an estimate already quoted. If a deposit is entered, status moves to Pending Deposit; if it's left blank/zero, the WO skips straight to In Progress — there's nothing to collect before work starts.
  4. Deposit (only when one was set) — once collected, status moves to In Progress — parts assignment, technician work, and time tracking (a later phase) happen from here.
Fees are collected at the register only — never from the Work Orders screenThe assessment fee is always required; the deposit is optional but, when set, is the same kind of cash-handling step, kept separate from whoever did the assessment. Until it's paid, the WO's own status (intake / pending_deposit) is an implicit "on hold" state: it shows up in the POS Hold Recall list alongside held sales and pending rentals, labeled WORK ORDER with the fee amount due, so a cashier can pick it up and process payment from the register — the same pattern a rental checkout's "pending" list uses. Opening the WO itself never shows a way to take the payment directly, no matter who's looking at it (including Administrators/Managers) — it just shows an "On hold — awaiting cashier" badge, pointing staff to the register instead.

Assessment queue (FIFO)

The Assessment Queue tab on the Work Orders list shows every WO in Assessed status — fee paid, waiting for someone to enter its estimate — sorted oldest first, with a queue position (#1, #2, …) and how long each has been waiting. Every other tab sorts most-recently-touched first; this one is a strict first-in-first-out line, so whoever enters estimates works top to bottom in the order items actually arrived.

Express same-day service is the sanctioned way to jump that line: from Enter Estimate, a supervisor (needs wo_signoff — the checkbox is hidden for anyone without it) can check ⚡ Express same-day service (+25%), which adds a 25% surcharge to the labor and consumables amounts being entered right there (a live preview shows the surcharged total before saving). It's a per-WO judgment call made at assessment time — how deep the queue is and how hard the job looks — not a customer-selectable option, and there's no separate approval step: checking the box and saving the estimate is the approval. An Express WO is flagged with an ⚡ Express badge everywhere it appears (the queue list, the WO detail title, the estimate line) so the surcharge stays visible for the rest of the WO's life.

Status history

Every transition — intake, fee paid, estimate entered, deposit paid, cancelled, plus free-form updates once a WO is In Progress, Awaiting Parts, or Awaiting Sign-Off — is logged with who did it and an optional comment. This is the running activity feed shown on the WO's detail view, so a customer-facing service rep can see exactly what's happened without digging through separate screens.

From Add Status Update, a WO that's In Progress, Awaiting Parts, or Awaiting Sign-Off can be freely moved between those three (e.g. pause it to Awaiting Parts while waiting on a backordered part, then move it back to In Progress once the part arrives), or set to Not Worth Fixing — a terminal outcome for when the item turns out not to be repairable or economical to fix once work has started. Not Worth Fixing locks the WO the same way Cancelled does: no further parts, description, or status edits.

Pickup window

Every WO gets a 30-day pickup-due date, counted from when it was opened (not from completion) — shown as its own badge on the WO list and detail view, independent of lifecycle status. A repair that takes longer than 30 days can show as approaching or past its pickup window before it's even finished; this is intentional, not a bug.

Cancelling

A WO can be cancelled (with a required reason) any time before the deposit is paid. Once it's In Progress or further along, it can no longer be cancelled — the deposit has been collected and work has started.

Parts

Parts (needs wo_assign_parts) can be identified and assigned from the retail catalog as soon as the WO is opened — no need to wait for the deposit, so a technician can pull or order parts while the estimate and deposit are still being sorted out with the customer. Editing stays open through every pre-completion status (Intake, Assessed, Pending Deposit, In Progress, Awaiting Parts, Awaiting Sign-Off); it locks once the WO reaches Complete, Cancelled, or Not Worth Fixing. Each "+ Add Part" line picks one of three types:

Both the Catalog Product field here and the Assessment Fee field at intake are searchable — type a few letters of the name (or SKU, for parts) and pick from the matching results, instead of scrolling a long list.

  • Catalog Product (in stock or elsewhere) — pick this for anything that's actually in the product catalog, even if this branch is out of stock. Sourcing works exactly like a retail quotation: if the WO's own branch has enough stock, the part is simply reserved from there; if not, the shortfall can be split across other branches (which auto-creates a real branch transfer) or left unassigned (which auto-creates a Purchase Request) — this happens automatically, there's nothing else to pick for "out of stock." Tax uses that product's own tax rate, snapshotted onto the line when it's added.
  • Not In Our Catalog — Order It — a free-text description and price for a part that isn't in the catalog at all yet, the same "Q" line a retail quotation uses for a custom item. It carries no branch-availability check; instead it's automatically flagged straight into the WO's Purchase Request on Confirm Parts, tagged Q with a link to that PR. Since it isn't a real catalog product yet, it's taxed at the company's default rate instead of a product-specific one. Once the resulting Purchase Order is received, linking it to a product (new or existing, from Purchasing's "+ Add to Inventory" on the unmatched line) flips the WO's line back over to that real product automatically — closing the loop the same way it does for a quotation's Q items. A brand-new product created this way is tagged non-inventory, same as a quote's.
  • Customer Brought Their Own — the customer supplied the part themselves. It's tracked on the WO (so the technician knows to use it) but is never sourced, never sent to Purchasing, and never charged (or taxed) — quantity and a description only, tagged Customer Supplied. Don't use this for a part the shop still needs to order — that's the "Not In Our Catalog" option above.

Each line shows a one-line hint under it explaining exactly what picking that type will do — read it before saving if you're not sure which one applies.

Save Parts records what's needed; Confirm Parts is the separate, explicit step that actually creates those transfers/purchase requests — the same "confirm what was drafted" split a quotation's Accept step uses. Each part line shows a live status badge (sourced from branch / transfer pending / PR pending / PO received / Q / Customer Supplied) that updates the same way a quotation's does once its linked purchase order comes in.

A catalog part that's pulled for a job but ends up not being installed — or one removed from the customer's machine and still resalable — can be sent back with Return on its line (any pre-completion status, needs wo_assign_parts). It asks how many to return and at what discounted resale price, then restocks that quantity under a distinct "(Used)" sibling product (its own SKU, price, and stock, tagged Used in Inventory) rather than mixing it back into the original product's new-condition stock — created automatically the first time a part of that product is returned, and topped up (at the latest asking price) on later returns. The original line keeps its assigned quantity for the record but shows how many were returned, and its charge on the work order drops to cover only what's left.

Tasks & time tracking

Once a WO is In Progress (needs wo_technician), it can be broken into tasks — a description, an allotted time in minutes, and an assigned technician. Each task has a Clock In / Clock Out button: clocking in starts a timer (and flips the task to In Progress); clocking out stops it. Actual time is the sum of every clock-in/clock-out pair on that task, so a technician can pause and resume across a break without losing what's already been logged. Actual time is shown in red once it exceeds the allotted minutes.

One timer per technicianA technician can only be clocked into one task at a time, anywhere in the system — clocking into a second task while still clocked into another is blocked with a message telling them to clock out of the first one first. This is what keeps the time data trustworthy enough to actually evaluate whether tasks are completed on time.

The ⏱ Active Tasks button on the Work Orders list header opens a live popup (refreshing every 15 seconds) showing every technician currently clocked in, which task, which WO, and elapsed vs. allotted time — a quick way for a supervisor to see who's working on what without opening each WO individually.

Skills & scheduling

A managed skill catalog ("Manage Skills" on the Work Orders list) lets you define what a technician can do (e.g. "Engine Repair", "Electrical"). Skills are assigned to technicians from their employee edit form, and can optionally be tagged as required on a task — the schedule board shows which skills each technician has next to unassigned tasks so it's obvious who's a fit.

The 📅 Schedule button opens a day-by-day board: pick a date, see every unscheduled task (with its required skills, if any) on one side, and everyone already assigned to that day on the other. Assigning a technician to a task for a given day doesn't lock anything — it's a planning aid, not a hard reservation, and can be removed and reassigned freely.

Sign-off, notification & pickup

Supervisor Sign-Off (needs wo_signoff) is only available once every task on the work order is marked Complete — signing off moves the WO to Complete and records who signed off and when.

Notify Customer then offers two ways to reach them: Send Email Notification (only shown if the customer has an email on file) sends a "ready for pickup" email and records it, or Mark as Called logs that they were reached by phone without sending anything. Either way, the WO moves to Awaiting Pickup.

Once the customer arrives, Process Final Payment is what closes the work order out — there's no separate "Mark Picked Up" step anymore. It's the same cashier-only, register-collected pattern as the assessment fee and deposit: the WO screen itself only ever shows an "On hold — awaiting cashier" badge, never a way to take the payment directly, and the WO appears in the POS Hold Recall list labeled WORK ORDER with the balance due, so a cashier picks it up and processes it from the register. The modal shows the full breakdown — estimate (labor + consumables) plus parts plus tax on both, less the deposit already paid — and charges exactly that balance. Tax is computed on the full estimate+parts amount regardless of the deposit — the deposit is just a prepayment credit, not something that partially "un-taxes" the sale. If the deposit already covers the final cost, the balance is JMD 0 and the button simply reads Complete Pickup with no payment method to pick — no transaction is created, but the WO still only advances to Picked Up through this one action.

The pickup-due badge keeps running the whole timeRemember the 30-day pickup window counts from when the WO was opened, not from completion — a work order can already show "Pickup overdue" on the Awaiting Pickup list the moment it lands there, if the repair itself took a while. That's expected, not a bug — it's meant to flag exactly that situation.

Invoice: print & email

🖨️ Print Invoice and ✉️ Email Invoice — titled Work Order Tax Invoice — are available from the WO detail view's footer, and from the cashier's Process Final Payment modal at the register — a full, letter-style invoice itemizing the assessment fee (plus its own tax, if any), estimate (labor + consumables, with any express surcharge noted), parts, the combined tax on estimate + parts, deposit paid, and balance due, each marked paid where applicable. It reflects whatever the WO has at the moment it's generated, so it's usable at any stage — not just once the WO is fully paid and picked up. Print opens it in a new tab and triggers the browser's print dialog; Email sends the same document (defaulting the recipient to the customer's email on file, if any) via the store's configured SMTP settings.

Promotions promotions

Cart- or catalog-wide discounts, either automatic or code-triggered.

  • A promotion is a percentage or fixed discount, optionally scoped to specific products or categories, with an optional date window and minimum purchase.
  • A product or category can only belong to one active promotion at a time — the system blocks adding it to a second one while the first is still active, so promotions never silently stack on the same item.
  • Bundle (Discount Type) — items sold together at a better price, e.g. a drill + 2 batteries. Add the products and how many of each make up one bundle (at least two items in total — "3 for" deals on a single product work too), then choose the pricing: a Discount % off the bundle's regular price, or a fixed Bundle Price. The form shows one bundle's regular price, what the customer pays and the saving as you type. It uses the same date range, times and recurrence as any promotion. At checkout every complete set in the cart gets the bundle pricing (two full sets = twice the discount; a partial set gets nothing), worked out from the prices actually in the cart. A line that already has a manual line-item discount doesn't count toward a bundle, so nothing is discounted twice — and unlike other promotions, a bundle doesn't stop its products getting a manual discount when they're sold on their own. Like every promotion, it competes with other qualifying promotions for the sale — only one applies at a time. Bundles have no Applies To or brand rules.
  • A promotion can have one or more codes, each with its own usage limit — retiring one code doesn't kill the whole promotion.
  • At checkout, promotions with no code attached are offered automatically; the best (largest discount) one is suggested first if more than one qualifies.
  • Start Time / End Time (optional) — narrows a promotion to a window of the day, e.g. a 2:00 PM-4:00 PM happy hour. Left blank, the promotion runs all day on any date it's otherwise eligible.
  • Recurrence — None (the default) runs the promotion once: Start Time/End Time apply only to the Start Date and End Date themselves, so a one-time window can still span several full days in between, e.g. a weekend sale from Friday 6:00 PM to Sunday 11:59 PM. Daily repeats the time window every day. Weekly repeats it on the day(s) of the week checked in the form (defaults to the Start Date's own weekday if none are checked). Monthly/Yearly repeat on the Start Date's day-of-month / month-and-day (clamped to the last day of a shorter month, e.g. a 31st-anchored monthly promo runs on the 28th/29th in February). Every recurrence is still bounded by the optional Start Date/End Date range.
  • Applies To → Specific Brands — scopes the promotion to one or more brands (set on each product — see Inventory). Pick which brands from the promotion's own Brands button on the list, the same way Items assigns products/categories. A brand can only be the inclusion set of one active promotion at a time, same exclusivity rule as products/categories.
  • Brand exclusion — independent of Applies To, the same Brands button can exclude a brand from a promotion no matter what it applies to (all items, a category, specific products, or another brand) — e.g. "15% off everything except Brand X." Multiple promotions can each exclude the same brand with no conflict, since exclusion never stacks a second discount on top.

Needs promotions_create to build a promotion, promotions_codes to manage its codes.

Discount Cards discount-cards

A simple flat percentage tied to a customer, presented as a physical card.

Set up card types here (a name plus a flat discount percentage), then assign a type and card number to a customer from their profile. There's no date window, no code, no product scoping — attaching a card-carrying customer to a cart just applies the discount automatically, and it always overrides a promo code rather than combining with one. A card type can't be deleted while any customer still references it — deactivate it instead to stop it applying without breaking customer history.

Cash Back Cards cash-back-cards

A loyalty-points-based rewards program, redeemable as cash off a future sale rather than a point-of-sale discount.

Set up card types here, each defining how a customer's existing loyalty points (earned automatically on every sale, see Customers) convert into redeemable cash back:

  • Points Threshold / Reward Amount — the conversion rate, expressed as whole chunks rather than a continuous rate: e.g. a threshold of 500 and a reward of $5 means every full 500 points accrued is worth $5, so 1,200 points is worth $10 (two full chunks), not $12.
  • Minimum Redeem Amount — optional; the customer's accrued cash back must reach at least this dollar amount before any of it can be applied. Leave at 0 to disable this gate.
  • Redemption Cooldown (days) — optional; once a customer redeems cash back, they can't redeem again until this many days have passed since that redemption. Leave at 0 to disable this gate. This only applies between redemptions — a customer's very first redemption only needs to clear the minimum amount gate, if set.

Both gates are independently optional, but when set, both must be satisfied at the same time before the Cash Back bar appears at checkout — see Point of Sale. Assign a type and an optional card number to a customer from their profile, the same way a discount card is assigned. A card type can't be deleted while any customer still references it — deactivate it instead to stop it applying without breaking customer history.

Inventory inventory

The retail product catalog and stock levels — rental items and services live in their own sections.

Products

Each product has a SKU, barcode, price, cost, tax rate, category, supplier, an optional free-text Brand, and a minimum-stock threshold used for low-stock alerts. Products can carry a photo (uploaded per product) and be organized by category. Brand is also what Promotions uses for brand-scoped and brand-excluded discounts.

Alongside the short Description (a one-liner used around the POS), each product — and each rental item, on its own form — has a Detailed Description box for the long-form copy shown on the website: features, specs, what's included. It's sent to the e-commerce site with the product (the online catalog feed and WooCommerce sync, where it becomes the product's main description and the short Description becomes WooCommerce's short description). Line breaks are kept; it's plain text, not HTML. It's also a long_description column in the Inventory and Rental Items CSV export, template and import — so descriptions can be bulk-loaded, and multi-line text survives a round trip (it's written as one quoted cell). An import only changes an item's detailed description when the file actually has that column mapped, so re-importing an older file without it leaves existing copy alone, and reversing an import batch restores it. The View button on each row of the Inventory and Rental Items lists opens a read-only card with the item's photo, pricing (or rental rates, deposit and accessories), stock, and both descriptions, with an Edit shortcut for anyone allowed to change it.

Units of measure

A retail item can be stocked in one unit and sold or ordered in others, each at its own price — rope stocked by the foot and sold by the yard, fabric by the metre, meat by the kilogram or pound, water by the single or the case.

The list of units. The Units button on Inventory (needs inventory_units, which starts on every group that can manage categories) lists the units you can use: Each, Case, Box, Pack, Dozen, Yard, Foot, Metre, Kilogram, Pound, Litre and Gallon to start with. Add your own, rename them, or make one inactive. Tick Decimals on a measured unit (yards, kg…) so it can be sold in part quantities like 2.5; counted units (Each, Case) stay whole numbers. A unit used by items can't be deleted, only made inactive.

On the item. The product form's Units of Measure box sets what stock is counted in (Each unless you pick another) — the item's Price, Cost and Stock are per that unit. + Add alternate unit adds another unit the item can be sold in: how many stock units are in one of it (1 Yard = 3 Foot; 1 Case = 24 Each), its price, and optionally its own barcode (it can't match any other item's barcode or SKU). Leave an alternate unit's price blank to only order it in that unit from suppliers. Manage Units in the box opens the units list without leaving the form.

Stock always moves in the stock unit: selling 2.5 yards of rope stocked in feet takes 7.5 feet off stock, and selling a case of 24 takes 24 singles.

  • POS — adding an item that has alternate units (Item Lookup, typing its number, or scanning its own barcode) asks which unit, showing each one's price. Scanning an alternate unit's own barcode (e.g. the case barcode) adds that unit without asking. Each unit is its own ticket line, named e.g. "Rope (Yard)" or "Spring Water (Case of 24)", and the stock check counts every line of the item together. Quantity accepts part quantities on a measured unit.
  • Returns and voids — returning or voiding a line puts its stock units back; a measured line can be part-returned (1.5 of 2.5 yd).
  • Retail and Special Project quotes — picking an item with alternate units asks which unit and fills in that unit's price (the branch-tiered price once a branch is chosen) and, on a Special Project, its cost. The line's unit dropdown changes it afterwards. Stock and branch sourcing are checked in the line's unit, and the unit carries through to the sale when the quote is converted or rung up.
  • Purchase orders — picking an item with alternate units asks which unit to order in (showing each one's cost); quantity and unit cost are then per that unit. Receiving adds the stock units to stock and sets the item's cost per stock unit. Part quantities can be ordered and received for a measured unit.
  • Layaway — each line has a unit dropdown for an item with alternate units; the layaway markup applies to that unit's price.
  • Reports — units sold and cost of goods are counted in stock units.

Items that used the earlier "Also sell by the case" box were moved over automatically to a Case alternate unit with the same size, price and barcode. Removing an alternate unit stops offering it; past sales, quotes and orders keep their unit.

Variations

For products that come in options (Size, Color, etc.), define variation types on the product, then add individual variants — each with its own SKU, price, and stock level. A sale against a specific variant deducts that variant's stock, not the parent product's.

Printing labels

The label icon on a product's row (needs inventory_print_labels) opens a barcode/price label print sheet for that item — pick a printer/label size, how many copies, and which fields (name, SKU, price, barcode, …) to include, then print from the popup that opens.

Stock — two numbers to know about

Every product has a single overall stock level, and separately a per-branch stock level once it's been touched by a branch-specific action (a sale, a received PO, a transfer, etc.). Most day-to-day stock changes update both automatically. If you ever see a stock number that looks "off" compared to what a branch report shows, this is usually why — see the note under Reports.

Bulk Assign to Branch button fixes the most common cause of this: a plain CSV import (below) only ever sets the overall stock level, never a branch-specific one, so right after a big import every branch-filtered view (and POS with a branch selected) shows 0 stock for everything just brought in, even though the overall total is correct. Click it to see how many active retail products currently have no branch assigned at all, pick one branch, and it copies each of those products' current overall quantity into that branch in one action. It only ever fills in this gap — a product that already has stock at any branch (or split across several) is left completely untouched, so it's safe to run more than once.

Bulk Tax Rate Change button overwrites the tax rate on every active product currently stocked at one branch — retail, rental, and non-inventory items alike, since a product is "at" a branch the same way Bulk Assign to Branch checks: it has a branch-specific stock row there. Pick a branch, see how many products that affects before committing, enter the new rate, and confirm — unlike Bulk Assign to Branch this always overwrites (there's no gap-only mode), and a product stocked at more than one branch has its single, global tax rate changed regardless of which of those branches you ran it for.

Adjusting stock and stock movements

Manual stock adjustments (needs inventory_adjust) and every automatic change (sales, receiving, transfers, cycle-count corrections) are logged as stock movements you can review per product, so you can always trace why a number changed.

Merging duplicate SKUs

Merge on a product's row (needs inventory_merge) consolidates it into another product you pick from a searchable list: the surviving product's stock (overall and per-branch) absorbs the duplicate's, and every sale, purchase order, quotation, work order, transfer, cycle count, bin assignment, and other record that named the duplicate is repointed to the survivor — so reports and history read as one SKU going forward. The duplicate itself is never deleted; it's deactivated and tagged Merged → [surviving SKU] on the inventory list, so anything that still shows its old name or SKU (a printed receipt, an old invoice) still traces back to a real record. This can't be undone from the UI — it would take a database-level fix — so double-check the target before confirming. Rental items and anything with active variations can't be merged (they don't offer the button); a variation-level duplicate should be cleaned up on the product's own Variations tab instead.

CSV import/export

Bulk-manage the catalog with a CSV template (inventory_template to download it, inventory_import to upload rows) you can download, edit, and re-import — useful for large catalog updates or migrating from another system. A separate template/import exists specifically for rental items. Rental Items also has an Import Accessories button for the previous system's accessories export (CSV or Excel), which only has an accessory name and an Active column (Yes/No, True/False or 1/0; blank counts as active): each becomes a rental accessory with a generated ACC-0001-style SKU, 0 stock, no branch and no rates, ready for counts to be assigned per location afterwards. A name that already exists as a rental accessory isn't duplicated — only its Active flag is updated — so the same file can be re-imported safely. To see just the accessories afterwards, set the type filter at the top of Rental Items to Accessories Only (or Exclude Accessories for everything else). Unlike regular rental items, which each belong to one branch, an accessory can be stocked at several locations at once: its edit form shows a Stock by Location box with a count per branch instead of the single Branch + Stock Qty pair, the list's Branch column shows each location's count, and its total stock is the sum. A rental checkout only draws on its own branch's count. To load counts in bulk, use Import Accessory Counts with a CSV or Excel file of Accessory (SKU or name), Branch and Quantity columns — each row sets that accessory's count at that branch (0 removes the location), so the file can hold as many locations per accessory as you need. A location's count can't be set below the units out on rental or held out of service there. The rental Export CSV writes one row per accessory location, so it re-imports through the regular rental Import without collapsing the locations. To turn an accessory back into a regular rental item, first set every location but one to 0. Your file's column headers don't need to match ours: right after you choose the file, a Map Import Columns screen lists every field we import (SKU and Name are required, everything else optional) next to a dropdown of the columns actually in your file, pre-matched wherever the names already line up — adjust anything wrong, leave the rest "Not mapped" to skip it, and continue. Rows are sent in batches of 25 with a progress bar; Cancel stops the next batch from starting, but whatever already posted stays imported (each batch is its own committed save, not one all-or-nothing operation). Before it starts, you're asked whether a SKU already in the system should be updated (the historical default) or skipped — useful for re-importing a stock list without clobbering products you've already cleaned up — and, on the same screen, which branch (if any) to assign the imported quantities to. Picking one sets every row's stock at that branch too, not just the overall total, so the products don't need a separate Bulk Assign to Branch pass afterward; leaving it on "All Branches total only" keeps the historical behavior. Since a retail product can legitimately be stocked at more than one branch, this only ever sets the chosen branch's own row — it never touches or removes stock the product already has at any other branch.

Every import is logged as a reviewable batch, opening a Review screen automatically once it finishes: counts of created/updated/skipped/error rows, and a breakdown of products the import touched that have questionable data — $0 price (worse when the item has real cost, since it would ring up free), negative stock, or no category/supplier/barcode. Export Issues CSV pulls just those flagged rows. Past imports stay listed under the Import History button (needs inventory_import_history) for the same review at any time.

Reverse This Import (also inventory_import_history) undoes a batch: products it created are deleted, and products it updated are restored to their values from just before the import. A created product is not deleted if it's since been touched by something real — a sale, a received PO, a stock adjustment, and so on — those are left alone and listed back to you with the reason, since deleting them would corrupt that real record. A batch can only be reversed once.

Independent of any specific import, the Missing Data button (needs inventory_missing_data) exports every active product with one of those same issues — useful for periodic catalog cleanup, not just right after a bulk import.

The Export CSV button (needs inventory_export) downloads whatever the current search/category/brand/branch filters show — the same column shape as the import template, so an export can be edited and re-imported. 📘 Catalogues (also inventory_export) builds a PDF product catalogue: give it a name and a description (both shown on the cover page), pick one or more categories and/or brands (with both, a product must match one of each — e.g. Drills by DeWalt), then untick any individual products to leave out, using the filter box and All/None to help. Show prices can be switched off for a catalogue without prices. Services and rental-only items aren't included. The PDF has a cover page with the company logo, the catalogue's name and description, the product count and date, then the products grouped by category in a three-across grid — picture, name, SKU, short description and price — with page numbers. Catalogues are saved, so ⬇ PDF on the list re-downloads one at any time with current prices and pictures; a product that's been made inactive drops out automatically. Product pictures in WebP or GIF format can't be placed in a PDF, so those products show a "No image" box — re-upload the picture as a JPG or PNG to include it. Scan Images (needs inventory_scan_images) matches image files already placed in uploads/products/ (named by SKU) to their products, for when images were dropped in directly rather than uploaded one at a time through the product form.

Quote-sourced items — the "Non-Inventory Items" tab

When a quotation includes a "Q" item — something quoted for a customer that isn't in the catalog yet — and its Purchase Order is received and linked to a new product (see Purchasing), that product is stocked and tracked normally, but it was bought to fulfill one specific quote, not for general resale. It's automatically kept out of the main product table above and listed instead under the Non-Inventory Items tab on this page.

Items there are still fully searchable and selectable when building a new quotation or purchase request, so ordering more of the same thing later works exactly like normal. Once you're ready to stock it for everyone — not just the customer it was originally quoted to — click Add to Warehouse Inventory on its row; it immediately moves into the main Inventory list as-is.

Not the same as ServicesThis is a different idea from the Services & Non-Inventory Items module below. A service/non-inventory-flagged product has stock tracking turned off permanently. An item on this "Non-Inventory Items" tab is a completely normal, stock-tracked product — it's just hidden from the main list until someone promotes it.

Categories & Brands

The Categories (needs inventory_categories) and Brands (needs inventory_brands) buttons manage those lists directly from Inventory — same underlying data Settings' own category/brand management uses, so a change either way shows up in both places. A category or brand still in use by any product can't be deleted; renaming a brand carries the new name through to every product tagged with it, since products.brand is a plain text copy, not a linked reference.

Permissions

inventory_add to create products, inventory_edit to change them, inventory_delete to remove them, inventory_adjust for manual stock corrections, inventory_print_labels to print labels, inventory_merge to merge duplicates, inventory_export / inventory_missing_data / inventory_template / inventory_import / inventory_import_history for the CSV workflow, inventory_scan_images to scan-match product images, inventory_categories / inventory_brands to manage those lists. Each one falls back to the parent inventory module flag if the security group never set it explicitly, so existing groups keep working exactly as before until you choose to narrow them.

Services & Non-Inventory Items services

Things you sell that aren't physical stock — labor, installation, warranties, etc.

These are ordinary products under the hood (same catalog, same /products data), just flagged as a service or non-inventory item, which turns off stock tracking for them entirely. They won't ever show up in low-stock alerts or stock reports, and selling one never touches a stock number.

One service, SN, is created automatically: it exists only to record a serial number on a ticket or quote (see Point of Sale → Recording serial numbers). Its SKU must stay SN for the serial prompt to appear; its name, price or active status can be changed like any other item.

Moving services between servers (Export / Import)

Export CSV on the Services screen downloads every service (active and inactive) with its SKU, name, description, category, unit, price, cost, tax rate and active flag. That same file can be loaded with Import on another server — e.g. to move services set up on the test server into production once they're ready. Excel files work too.

  • Rows are matched by SKU: an existing service with that SKU is updated, anything else is created as a new service.
  • Categories are matched by name; a category that doesn't exist on the receiving server yet is created automatically.
  • A row whose SKU already belongs to a regular inventory or rental item is skipped and listed as an error — it's never converted into a service.
  • A blank tax rate falls back to the store's default; a blank Active column means active.

Purchasing purchasing purchase_requests

Getting new stock from suppliers, from an internal request through to receiving it.

Purchase Requests (PR) — the ask

Staff request items either for resale or for internal use (consumables that won't track stock). A request line can point at an existing catalog product — picked from a searchable box (type a few letters of the name or SKU) rather than scrolling the full catalog — or just be typed in by name with an optional source URL, handy for something not in the catalog yet. Department is picked from a list — type to search it, and if the department isn't there choose + Add “…” (or press Enter) to add it to the list for everyone. Requests flow draft → submitted → approved/rejected (needs pr_create / pr_approve — approving and rejecting are refused without pr_approve); approving signs the request with your approval signature, shown under Approved by on the request, and an approved request converts 1:1 into a Purchase Order (pr_convert). A line auto-created from an accepted quotation carries that quote line's discount forward as a small tag, visible here and again on the resulting PO — see Quotations' Per-line discount note.

Tax on a request. A request in the local currency adds local tax (the GCT rate from Settings); one in a foreign currency has none. Ticking Online Purchase (overseas orders — it also adds a product URL box per item) removes local tax even in the local currency. For a local purchase where some items are tax exempt, tick No Tax on those item lines — they're left out of the tax and show a Tax exempt tag on the request — or tick Tax Exempt (all items) to mark every line at once. When the request is converted, those lines (or every line of an online request) become PO lines with no tax.

Documents on a request. Attach supplier quotes, a brief or specification, or anything else backing the request. On the request form, pick a document type and choose one or more files under 📎 Documents — they upload when you save. Afterwards, + Attach Document in the request's Attachments section (on its View screen) adds more at any stage. PDF, Word, Excel, images and text files up to 20 MB each. Requests with documents show a 📎 count in the list, and the PO converted from the request notes how many documents are on it. Documents can be removed while the request is a draft, submitted or rejected; once it's approved they stay on the record. Like PO and quotation attachments, they can only be downloaded while signed in.

Services and other non-physical items. For something that isn't a physical item — a promotional video, design work, a subscription — set the line's Item Type to Service. The product search is cleared and locked (a service is never an inventory item); type a short name in Item Name (e.g. "Promotional video") and describe the scope in the Details line that opens underneath — deliverables, revision rounds, format, deadline. Enter the quantity (usually 1) and estimated cost as normal. Service lines show a Service tag on the request and on the PO, their details print on the PO sent to the supplier, and they never change stock. Receive the line on the PO once the work is delivered; that's what completes the PO and, for an A/P vendor, creates the bill. Service lines can't be added to inventory or processed as damaged, and aren't allowed on a Fuel / supplier-priced request.

Fuel / supplier-priced requests. For purchases the supplier prices on delivery — fuel, for example — choose the Fuel / Supplier-priced request type. Pick each item from inventory and enter the maximum quantity (part quantities such as litres are allowed); there's no cost to enter. Once the request is approved and signed, Print Authorization or Email Authorization on the request produces the signed Authorization to Pump for the supplier: the items, maximum quantities and approver's signature, with no prices (by email it can go as a PDF). Convert the request to a PO as usual; the PO starts at the authorized quantities with no cost, and each line shows its max. When the supplier's invoice comes back, edit the draft PO to the quantity supplied and the invoiced cost, and attach the invoice with document type Supplier Invoice. The PO can't be sent for approval or approved until every line has a cost and the invoice is attached; a quantity over the authorized maximum is flagged in red and asks you to confirm, but isn't blocked. Its supplier invoice can't be removed after the PO leaves Draft.

Approval signatures. Approving a purchase order, a purchase request, a Special Project or a commission (one at a time, or Approve All Pending, which puts your signature on each record it approves) signs it with the approver's signature. The first time you approve anything you draw your signature once; it's saved to your login, and every later approval shows it and applies it with one click. Draw a new signature in the Approve dialog replaces your saved one for that and future approvals. Each approved document keeps the signature it was approved with, so changing yours later never alters documents already approved. Commission records show who approved them, with the signature, in the Status column. The signature appears on the document's own screen and on its printed, emailed and PDF copies (POs and Special Project quotations — purchase requests have no printed copy), and like other signatures it can only be opened while signed in — emails carry it embedded.

Purchase Orders (PO) — the supplier-facing order

  1. Draft — built directly, from a converted PR, or sent back here by a rejection. Fully editable: items (qty/cost), supplier, branch, dates, and notes can all be changed while a PO is in Draft. The supplier box is type-to-search (as are the supplier/vendor boxes on products, purchase requests, bills and vendor payments), and the item picker is searchable and scoped to whichever supplier is selected — pick the supplier first to see their products.
  2. Sent — formally submitted for approval.
  3. Approved or Rejected (needs purchasing_approve) — the decision on a submitted PO.
  4. Receiving (needs purchasing_receive) — mark items as arrived; this can happen over multiple partial deliveries. A PO must be Approved before anything can be received on it — the Receive button only appears once it is (a PO already part-received can still be finished). Receiving updates stock (both overall and at the PO's branch) and refreshes the product's cost to the latest price paid. Each receipt also creates an Accounts Payable bill for what was received, for vendors set to sync with A/P.

A PO can also be marked cancelled, and items received damaged can be logged separately to reverse the stock that was just added. You can attach files to a PO — invoices, packing slips — up to 20MB each.

Tax and vendor discounts on PO lines

Every PO line has its own tax option and vendor discount. Tax is either GCT (local) — the store's own sales tax rate from Settings — or No tax (overseas) for a vendor that doesn't charge it, so one PO can mix both. New lines start on the supplier's default: GCT if the supplier is marked Local Supplier, no tax otherwise; Set every line to above the items flips them all at once. The vendor discount is what the vendor takes off that line — type dollars (50) or a percentage (10%); a percentage keeps following the line if you change its quantity or cost. It comes off before tax and can't be more than the line's cost (or over 100%). The PO shows Items, Vendor discounts, Subtotal, GCT and Total, and the same breakdown prints on the approved PO document. The GCT rate is saved on each line when it's priced, so a later change to the store's tax rate doesn't alter an existing PO. A PO converted from a Purchase Request keeps the PR's tax decision (GCT when the PR is in the local currency, except an Online Purchase PR or a line marked No Tax). On receiving, each received unit is billed to A/P at its discounted cost plus its line's GCT, and the product's cost is updated to the discounted unit cost.

Approvals

The Approvals tab (visible with purchasing_approve) is a tray of every PO currently Sent and awaiting a decision — draft POs aren't shown there since their requester hasn't submitted them yet. From the tray, or from a PO's own detail view, an approver can:

  • Approve — signs the PO with your approval signature (see Approval signatures below); it's shown on the completed PO document. Approving clears any earlier rejection note.
  • Reject with a required reason — the PO drops back to Draft, the reason shows as a banner on the PO (who rejected it and why) until the requester edits it and marks it Sent again, which clears the banner.

A PO's detail view also links straight through to whatever document drove the purchase: the Purchase Request it was converted from (if any), and per line, the specific Quotation or Work Order its "Q" item traces back to — so an approver can check the original context without hunting for it separately.

Once approved, Print and Email PO on the detail view produce the same completed document — supplier, items, total, and the approver's captured signature with their name and the approval date/time. Email PO has an Attach as PDF option: ticked, the supplier gets a short cover email with the PO attached as a PDF (same contents and signature); unticked, the PO is in the email body.

A PO can carry the vendor's own order/quote number (e.g. an Amazon order #) separate from its own PO number — set it when creating the PO or edit it later from the PO detail view. Alongside it, a Freight Forwarding panel tracks orders that ship through a freight forwarder before reaching a branch: Pending → Received by Forwarder → Forwarded to Us, each stamped with a timestamp, plus an optional freight tracking number and notes. This status is independent of the PO's own approval/receiving status above.

Items with no product yetA PO line for something not yet in your catalog can be "linked" to a product after the fact — either an existing one or a brand-new one created on the spot — and any quantity already received retroactively applies to its stock. If the line traces back to a quotation's "Q" item, the newly created product is automatically tagged non-inventory and shows under Inventory's Non-Inventory Items tab rather than the main catalog list, since it was bought for that one quote.

Suppliers suppliers

The vendor directory purchase orders are placed against.

The supplier form is laid out like a typical legacy vendor screen: Vendor Number (type your own, e.g. V0002, or Auto-assign the next SUP- number), Name Type (Business/Person) and Name at the top, then three tabs:

  • Main — salutation, first/last name, Contact 1 and Phone 1, Category, Terms code, Ship-via code, A/P vendor #, Vendor's ref # (your account number with them), Voucher default, Sync with A/P, plus the Local Supplier flag (default tax applies to purchases) and status.
  • Address — Address 1–3, city, state/parish, zip, country, Comments 1–3 and notes.
  • Contacts — Contact 1 and Contact 2, each with phone, fax, email and URL, and which email receipts/reports go to.

Terms code sets when that vendor's bills fall due (the first number in it is the day count — "Net 30", "N30"; COD/Prepaid are due immediately). Sync with A/P (on by default) together with Voucher default decides whether receiving a PO from this vendor creates its Accounts Payable bill automatically — turn Sync off, or set Voucher default to "Not vouchered", to enter that vendor's bills by hand instead. Category, Ship-via, A/P vendor # and the contact/receipt-email details are kept for reference only.

Import (needs suppliers_add) loads vendors from a CSV, a tab-separated text file, or an Excel workbook (.xls or .xlsx — the first sheet is used), including files saved as "Unicode" text. A Map Vendor Import Columns screen matches your file's columns to every field above (headers from a typical legacy vendor export match automatically; only Name is required). Rows whose vendor number already exists, or whose name matches an active supplier, are likely duplicates — skipped by default, or imported anyway (a clashing number is then replaced with a new SUP- number). Template downloads the column layout with an example row. Every import is logged under Import History, where an import can be reviewed and reversed (needs suppliers_delete): vendors it created that haven't been used yet are deleted outright (so their numbers can be re-imported), while any already used on a purchase order, product, or purchase request are deactivated instead and listed on the review screen.

Users with Accounts Payable access also see a Balance Owed column (red when part of it is overdue) — click it for that vendor's statement.

Needs suppliers_add / suppliers_edit / suppliers_delete for the respective actions.

Branch Transfers transfers

Moving stock between two of your locations.

  1. Request (needs transfers_create) — the branch that needs stock picks the branch to request it from, the branch it's for, and the items and quantities. The transfer shows as requested; no stock moves yet.
  2. Review & Send (needs transfers_approve — give this to the inventory team) — the sending branch sees each item's requested quantity next to what it has in stock, enters the quantity it's actually sending (0 to leave an item out), and sends. Stock is deducted from the sending branch at this point and the transfer shows as sent. The original request is kept, so the transfer shows Requested, Sent and Received side by side.
  3. Dispatch (optional, needs transfers_pickup) — marks it picked up from the source and in transit.
  4. Receive (needs transfers_dropoff) — the requesting branch adds the sent quantities to its stock; this can be done in partial batches over more than one delivery.

Request notifications — staff with transfers_approve are told when a branch requests stock from one of their branches (the branches they're assigned to; every branch if they have none — they aren't told about their own requests). A notice pops up in the bottom-right corner within about a minute, with Review & Send and Pick List buttons, and stays until dismissed; requests that came in while they were signed out show when they sign in. A red count of requests still waiting to be sent shows on the Warehouse menu item and the Branch Transfers tile until each is sent or cancelled.

Pick List — a requested or sent transfer has a 🗒 Pick List button (on its row, in its detail view, and on the Review & Send screen) that prints each item with its bin location(s) at the sending branch, sorted by bin so it can be picked in one walk, the quantity to pick (requested, or sent once it's been sent), what's in stock (while it's still a request — short items are in red), a Picked ✓ box per line and Picked by / Checked by sign-off lines.

A request can be cancelled before it's sent with nothing to undo. Cancelling a sent transfer restores whatever hadn't yet been received back to the source branch. Transfers created automatically — when a multi-branch quotation is accepted, or a work order needs parts from another location — skip the request step and are sent straight away.

The Request Transfer product picker is searchable — type a few letters of the name or SKU and pick from the matching results, instead of scrolling a long list — and works for rental items too (tagged [Rental] in the results) — see Rentals for how a rental item's currently-checked-out units are excluded from what's offered.

Warehouse Management warehouse cycle-counts shipping

Physical storage layout, inventory audits, and outbound shipments.

Zones & bins

Set up named zones per branch, then individual storage bins within each zone (with an optional capacity). Assign products to specific bins with a quantity (the product picker is searchable — type a few letters of the name or SKU rather than scrolling the full catalog), marking one bin as "primary" if a product lives in more than one spot. A bin's assigned quantity is automatically kept in sync with that branch's stock level everywhere stock moves — receiving, shipping, transfers, layaway, cycle counts — so you never have to update it by hand.

Cycle Counts

A physical inventory audit. Start a session scoped to all products, a category, or a specific bin — it snapshots what the system currently expects for each item. Export the sheet to CSV for an offline count, then import the actual counted quantities back in; the system computes the variance per line.

Import Counts takes a CSV or Excel file — either the session's own exported sheet with Counted Qty filled in, or a plain scanner file with a SKU (or barcode) column and a Qty column; add a Bin Code column when a SKU is counted in more than one bin. Before anything is saved it shows how many rows matched, the total quantity, and every row it couldn't match and why. When several scanners count the same branch, import each scanner's file separately with Add to existing counts: an item counted on more than one scanner (front shelf and back room, say) gets the quantities added together, and importing the same file twice is caught before it doubles the counts. Use Replace counts for a recount. Every import is listed on the session with its scanner name, file and who imported it, and any one can be Undone without affecting the others. Typing a count on screen and pressing Save Counts replaces that line's count.

Committing the session applies only the lines that actually differ, adjusting stock and bin quantities and logging each change for audit purposes — once committed, a session is locked. For a full or initial count, tick Count uncounted lines as 0 in the commit dialog so anything nobody scanned is set to zero; leave it unticked for a partial count. Needs cyclecounts_create to run a session, cyclecounts_approve to commit it.

Shipping

Create a shipment (a set of items going out to a customer or another location) — its item picker is searchable, type a few letters of the name or SKU rather than scrolling the full catalog — print a pick list for warehouse staff and a shipping label once it's packed, then progress it: draft → shipped (deducts stock) → delivered. A shipment can also be created directly from an online order that's ready to fulfill, and cancelling a shipment that had already shipped restores the stock it took.

Customers customers

Customer profiles, purchase history, and account status.

  • Standard contact info, loyalty points (earned automatically on every sale), and total lifetime spend.
  • Credit accounts — flag a customer as a credit customer to let them buy on account; see Accounts Receivable for how that's collected and enforced.
  • Discount card assignment (type + card number) lives on this profile — see Discount Cards.
  • Customer filter — next to the search box on the Customers list: Credit, Cash, Rental Approved (rental customers who can rent today — preapproved, or with every required document on file) or Other (everyone who can't rent yet). It combines with the search.
  • Duplicate check — adding, editing and importing a customer all refuse one whose phone or email already belongs to another active customer. Phones are compared number by number: a field holding two numbers (876-975-4271/618-0223), a 7-digit local number (treated as 876), a leading 1 and a trailing extension digit are all handled, and placeholder numbers like 876-000-0000 are ignored rather than treated as a match. Emails are compared ignoring case. So importing the old system's customer list skips anyone already on file — including existing rental-preapproved customers — and the import's results list which existing customer each skipped row matched.
  • Find Duplicates (needs customers_merge) — lists groups of active customers who share a phone number (compared number by number, as above) or an email, with each one's sales, rentals, legacy invoices, balance and when they were added (and whether by an import). Pick the customer to keep — pre-selected is the one with the most history, preferring a rental-approved record — and Merge into Kept Customer: the others' sales, returns, payments, legacy invoices, quotes, rentals, layaways, work orders, shipments, CRM records and contact persons move to the kept customer; balances, loyalty points and total spent are added together; details the kept customer is missing (email, address, rental ID and references, discount/cash-back card…) are filled in, a second phone number is kept alongside the first and a different email goes into the notes; and rental, tax-exempt and credit standing carry over. The merged customers are deactivated and marked as merged, not deleted, so old receipts still show them. A summary of what will move is shown before you confirm; merging can't be undone. Not Duplicates hides a group (e.g. family members sharing a phone) — it reappears only if another customer joins it. Note: RetailPOS Books keeps a merged customer's earlier invoices under that customer.
  • Individual or Business — chosen at the top of the customer form. An individual has a First and Last Name; a business has a Business Name and Business Address instead, plus a Contact Persons list — at least three rows of name, job title, phone and email (+ Add Contact for more; unused rows are left blank, but a row with details needs a name). The business name is what shows on receipts, invoices, statements and searches, and the customer list marks businesses with a Business tag. Switching an existing customer from Individual to Business starts the business name from their current name. Imports and other integrations that don't send a type leave a customer's type, business name and contacts as they are. The customer Import can create businesses too: map Business Name (and optionally Customer Type — Individual/Business — and up to three contacts' name, job title, phone and email) instead of First/Last Name; a row with a business name and no first/last name is taken as a business. The template has a business example row, and Export writes the same columns.
  • Customer Category — a classification (Individual, Company, Contractor, ...) set up in Settings, each with its own discount percentage. It's separate from the Cash/Credit Customer Type field above — that one governs accounts-receivable behavior, this one is just a tag with an optional automatic discount. It applies at POS checkout the same way a discount card does, but only when the customer has no discount card assigned (a card takes priority).
  • Cash back card assignment (type + card number) also lives on this profile — see Cash Back Cards.
  • Rental compliance fields also live here and gate whether the customer can start a new rental: a photo ID (type, number, and a scanned copy), an uploaded proof-of-address document (image or PDF), and three references — the main one (Reference 1) also captures an address and its own scanned photo ID upload; References 2 and 3 are name/phone/relationship only. Preapproved (needs customers_rental_preapprove) lets a rental customer through despite missing any of the above — meant for someone migrated from a previous system whose old export never carried this data, so it can only be switched on for a customer that a CSV import (below) created. A customer added by hand never gets the checkbox — they need the full ID, proof of address and references before they can rent. Revoking a preapproval (unchecking it) works on any customer. It doesn't make the gaps disappear: the customer still shows on Missing Rental Info (below) and gets a warning banner when selected for a New Rental, so staff still know to go collect what's missing — it just stops that from blocking a rental today. Records who approved it and when underneath the checkbox; unchecking it clears that record, and re-checking it later stamps a fresh approver/date rather than restoring the old one. A security group without the permission doesn't see the checkbox at all — just a read-only note of the customer's current status — and any rental_preapproved value it sends elsewhere (an edit save, a CSV import row) is silently ignored rather than applied.
  • A customer's full transaction history is viewable from their profile.
  • Duplicate check on creation — adding a new customer checks active customers for a match on email, phone, or full name before saving. A match pauses the save with a picker: View jumps to the existing customer's profile, or Create New Customer Anyway proceeds and saves a separate record regardless. No match means the customer is created immediately, same as before. (Editing an existing customer isn't checked — only new ones.) The same check runs when converting a CRM lead into a customer, and on every row of a CSV import (below).
  • CSV import (needs customers_import) — Template downloads the expected column headers with an example row; Import uploads a CSV (or tab-separated text, or an Excel .xls/.xlsx workbook) of new customers. As with Inventory's import, a Map Import Columns screen appears first so your file's own headers can be matched to our fields (First Name and Last Name are required — or Business Name, for businesses) instead of needing to match exactly. Since there's no natural key to upsert by (unlike Inventory's SKU-based import), every row runs through the same duplicate check as adding one customer by hand; up front you choose whether rows matching an existing active customer are skipped (the default) or imported anyway — there's no per-row Link option in a bulk import, since it can't pause to ask. Export (needs customers_export) downloads active customers in the same column shape, so an export can be edited and re-imported. Every import is logged as a reviewable, reversible batch — Import History (needs customers_import_history) lists past imports with created/skipped/error counts; opening one shows exactly which rows were skipped and which existing customer they matched, plus a Reverse This Import button (also customers_import_history) that deactivates every customer that batch created (same as deleting them one at a time — nothing is hard-deleted). The template's columns include the text half of Rental compliance (above) — flag a row is_rental_customer, plus photo ID type/number, all three references, and rental_preapproved — so rental customers migrated from a previous system can be bulk-loaded with whatever data the old export actually has and flagged as usable right away; the three scanned documents (ID, address proof, Reference 1's ID) have no bulk path and still need uploading one customer at a time. The rental_preapproved column only takes effect for someone importing with customers_rental_preapprove — without it, that column is silently ignored and every imported row comes in un-preapproved instead of failing the import.
  • Missing Rental Info (needs customers_missing_rental_info) — lists every active Rental Customer whose compliance record is still incomplete, with exactly which piece is missing per customer, a Preapproved/Blocked status column, and a shortcut into that customer's Edit form. The same eligibility check a new rental agreement enforces (Rental compliance, above), surfaced as a working list instead of a per-rental error — mainly useful right after a CSV import of legacy rental customers to track down what still needs collecting (especially the document uploads a CSV can't carry). A Preapproved row can already be rented to; a Blocked one can't, until its missing fields are added or — for a customer created by an import — someone with customers_rental_preapprove checks Preapproved on its profile.
  • Export Balances (needs customers_export_balances) — a separate, report-shaped CSV export (unrelated to the re-importable Export above): All Customers, Retail Only, or Rental Only (the Rental Customer flag), including every customer regardless of active status. Alongside contact info it reports AR balance (what the customer currently owes), credit note total (unredeemed store credit currently owed to the customer), and status (Active / Inactive / Blocked). Both money columns are derived from the same account_balance field — a positive balance is AR, a negative one is store credit — see Accounts Receivable for how a return or rental deposit resolved as a "credit note" moves that balance negative.
  • From a customer's transaction-history view, Print (needs customers_print_history) opens a clean, printable statement of that customer's transactions and payments for the selected date range.

Needs customers_add / customers_edit / customers_delete; extending or adjusting credit terms needs customers_credit. Each button-grid action above (CSV import, export, import history/reverse, Missing Rental Info, Export Balances, printing a customer's statement) is gated by its own sub-permission and simply omitted — not just disabled — for a security group that lacks it, the same pattern used in Point of Sale and Inventory. ID scan, reference ID, and address-proof document uploads on a customer's profile fall under customers_edit. The scanned files themselves (and captured rental/PO signatures) can only be opened while signed in — a copied link opened in a logged-out browser goes to the login screen first. Emailed rental invoices, agreement summaries and approved POs carry their signatures embedded in the email rather than linking back to the server. Viewing a customer's own profile and transaction history has no separate gate — only these editing/reporting/bulk actions do. customers_rental_preapprove is separate from customers_edit on purpose — it's the one rental-compliance control that overrides a real check instead of just recording data, so it isn't handed out automatically to Administrator/Manager the way most sub-permissions are; only Administrator has it until a store explicitly grants it via Security Groups.

Accounts Receivable accounts

Managing customers who buy on credit instead of paying up front.

  • Each credit customer carries a running account balance and a credit limit; a credit sale adds to the balance instead of collecting cash.
  • Payments toward a customer's account are received only at the POS, by cashiers (pos_receive_payment — Security Groups → Customer Payments, a separate tick not included with POS access; it starts on the Cashier and Senior Cashier groups). Receive Payment on the POS button grid needs an open cash drawer: pick the customer (only customers with a balance are listed; the one on the ticket is pre-selected), then choose Open payment (on account) — the amount comes off the balance without being applied to any invoice — or Pay specific invoices — tick the invoices being paid (each defaults to its full balance due; change it for a part payment) and the payment total is their sum. Choose how they paid (cash, card, cheque or direct deposit; anything but cash needs its approval code / cheque number / deposit reference). A payment can't be more than the balance owed. Open payments still waiting to be applied are listed on the same screen with Apply to Invoices, which matches them to invoices later without moving any money. Receivables representatives can also allocate open payments and move a payment between invoices on the same account from RetailPOS Books (each customer's account page there) — that never changes the amount received or creates a payment, and every allocation change, from either app, is recorded with who made it. Every payment is recorded against the cashier's drawer session and counted by tender in that drawer's reconciliation. The Accounts screen keeps the aging report (with an Unapplied Payments column), payment history (showing any amount not yet applied) and statements, but no longer records payments.
  • Auto credit-check — every time a payment is received (open payments not yet applied count against the oldest invoices first) (and periodically for the whole customer list), the system checks how long a customer's oldest credit sale still owing (or unpaid legacy invoice) has been outstanding against their payment terms, and whether their balance is over their credit limit. Exceed it, and the account is automatically blocked from further credit sales until the balance is brought current — this happens without anyone manually flagging the account.
  • Block box — a credit customer's form has a Block account checkbox showing whether they can buy on account, and why. It's ticked automatically when the account is overdue (its oldest invoice still owing is past the customer's terms) or its balance is over the credit limit — checked again after every credit sale and payment — and unticks by itself once payments are up to date and the balance is back within the limit. Staff with customers_credit_block (Security Groups → Credit Control, a separate tick that isn't included with Customers access) can also change it, giving a reason each time (recorded with their name and the date): unticking an overdue or over-limit account lets the customer keep buying on credit until they're up to date and within the limit again — after that, falling behind blocks them as normal; ticking an up-to-date account blocks it by hand, and it stays blocked, payments or not, until someone with the permission unticks it. Everyone else sees the box but can't change it. A blocked customer is refused credit sales at the POS and on rentals, with the reason shown. A credit sale or rental that would take the balance over the credit limit is refused before it's rung up, showing the credit still available — unless the account has been unblocked by hand, which lets it go over.
  • Write-offs for balances that will never be collected need accounts_writeoff.
  • Legacy invoices — invoices still owed from the old system. The accounts team enters each one in RetailPOS Books (New Invoice → tick Legacy invoice from the old system); Books sends it to the customer's account here, where it's added to their balance and shown on a Legacy Invoices tab of the account view (old invoice number, date, due date, balance). From then on it's collected like any credit invoice: Record Payment and write-offs apply to it (oldest first unless you allocate by hand — it's marked Legacy in the list), it counts in the aging report and statements, and an unpaid one past the customer's terms triggers the auto credit-check block. Payments and write-offs taken here flow back to Books on its next sync. Legacy invoices never count as sales, revenue, commissions or drawer takings. The customer must have credit enabled here first, or Books shows the invoice as "not in the POS yet" and retries on its next sync. Voiding one (entered by mistake) is done from Books, and only while nothing has been paid against it. Books connects with an API key that has the Accounts Receivable — Write legacy invoices scope (Settings → API Keys).

Accounts Payable payables

What you owe your vendors, when it's due, and what you've paid.

  • Bills — each one is an amount a vendor has invoiced you, with the vendor's own invoice number, bill date and due date (bill date + the vendor's terms, editable). A bill gets created three ways:
    • Automatically when a PO is received — every receipt (full or partial) becomes a bill for the value just received (quantity × unit cost, plus the PO's tax in proportion), dated that day and due per the vendor's terms. This happens for vendors with Sync with A/P on and Voucher default not set to "Not vouchered" (see Suppliers). Add the vendor's invoice number to the bill once their invoice arrives.
    • From a PO — the PO detail view has an Accounts Payable panel showing received vs. billed vs. not yet billed, with Create Bill prefilled with the unbilled amount (for vendors whose bills aren't automatic, or after voiding one).
    • By hand with Enter Bill — for anything not bought on a PO (utilities, rent, services).
  • The same vendor invoice number can't be entered twice for one vendor, so an invoice can't accidentally be paid twice.
  • Pay Vendor (needs payables_payments) — pick the vendor, date, method (cheque, bank transfer, cash, card, other) and cheque/reference number, then apply the payment across any of their unpaid bills. Typing the payment amount spreads it over their bills oldest-due first; adjust any bill's share and the total follows. A payment has to be fully applied — it can't exceed what's owed on the bills it's applied to.
  • A bill shows as Open, Partial, Overdue (past its due date with a balance), Paid or Void.
  • Aging by Vendor — what each vendor is owed, split into Current (not yet due), 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and 90+ days past due. Click a vendor for their statement: every bill and payment in date order with a running balance.
  • Voiding (needs payables_void, with a reason) — voiding a payment makes the bills it paid owing again; a bill can only be voided once no payments are applied to it (void those payments first). Nothing is deleted — void items stay visible, just not counted.
  • Entering and editing bills needs payables_bills; viewing needs payables. When this module was added, only the Administrator group and any group named for payables received it — grant it to others in Security Groups.

CRM & Sales Pipeline crm

Tracking prospective business, separate from walk-in retail sales.

  • Leads (crm_leads) — a prospective contact, which can be converted into an opportunity once qualified. The Leads tab can be searched, filtered by status/salesperson, and sorted (Newest First, or alphabetically A-Z/Z-A by name) — a salesperson sees only their own leads in that list, per the note below.
  • Convert to Customer checks for a possible duplicate first — any active customer sharing the lead's email, phone, or full name — before creating a new record. A match pauses the conversion with a picker: Link attaches the lead to that existing customer instead of creating a new one, or Create New Customer Anyway proceeds and creates a separate record regardless. No match means the customer is created immediately, same as before.
  • Opportunities (crm_opportunities) — a deal in progress, moved through pipeline stages; can link directly to a quotation, so a sales-driven deal and its formal price quote stay connected. Its Lead field records how the deal came in — Bid, Email, WhatsApp, Website or Site visit — picked from a type-to-search list; if the type isn't listed, press + (or choose + Add new…), type it and press Enter to add it to the list for everyone. The lead type shows on the pipeline card and the opportunity's detail view. A new opportunity is always assigned to whoever creates it — there's no picker on the create form, just an "assigned automatically" note — and once created, only someone with sales_manager can hand it to a different salesperson, via the Reassign button on the opportunity's detail view or edit form (same pattern as reassigning a quote). Without that permission, the edit form shows who it's assigned to as read-only text.
  • Activities — calls, meetings, follow-up tasks logged against a lead or opportunity, each markable complete (or reopened). An activity can also attach straight to a customer with no lead or opportunity needed.
  • Rentals feed into a customer's Activities automatically — checkout, return, and cancellation each log a completed entry, so a rep can see a customer's rental history right in CRM. An overdue rental logs as an open (not completed) activity with the original due date, so it shows up in the Activities tab's Overdue bucket as something to follow up on — checked roughly every 30 minutes, not instantly.
  • A CRM-specific dashboard summarizes pipeline health separately from the main Dashboard.
  • An employee flagged Salesperson (see Employees) only sees leads assigned to them, opportunities/activities they own, and their own slice of the CRM dashboard — trying to open another rep's lead or opportunity directly is blocked. For Opportunities specifically, "owned" means either created it themselves or had it handed to them by a sales_manager reassignment — nothing else can move an opportunity into or out of their pipeline.
  • Targets tab — Hourly, Daily, Weekly, and Monthly sales targets for each employee flagged Salesperson, shown next to their current sales for that same period as a progress bar. Setting or changing a target needs sales_manager (the "Set Targets" table at the top of the tab is hidden without it) — a plain salesperson can see their own bars but not edit any target, their own included. Current sales means transactions in the current hour/day/week/month credited to them: the ticket's Salesperson (transactions.salesperson_id — the salesperson who started the ticket, the one picked at the POS, or one set later by a sales manager through Transactions → Change Salesperson), else whoever started the ticket (created_by), else the cashier who rang it up (employee_id). Totals are worked out from the sales history each time, so sales made before a target was set still count for the current period — and commission on a POS sale goes to the same person. A sale completed from a quote was started by the quote's original salesperson, so a reassigned quote still credits them. A bar with no target set yet shows an empty track instead of 0%. Once a target exists, the bar's fill goes green at 100%+, amber from 50–99%, and red below 50%.

Commissions commissions

Paying staff a cut of what they sell.

  • Define commission plans (needs commissions_plans) — a rate structure — and assign them to specific employees.
  • A commission record is created automatically whenever an assigned employee completes a sale or wins a CRM opportunity — no manual entry needed for the common case. A POS sale's commission goes to whoever the sale is credited to (its Salesperson, else whoever started the ticket, else the cashier), and moves with it if a sales manager changes the Salesperson afterwards (see Transactions); paid commission never moves.
  • A plan's Applies To setting scopes it to POS sales, quotes, CRM deals, rentals, or all of them — a plan only earns commission on the category(ies) it's scoped to, so assigning someone a POS-only plan doesn't automatically start paying them on rentals too.
  • Rentals are commissioned on a delay, not at booking — the risk being commissioned against is different depending on how the rental was paid. A cash/card-paid rental earns commission once the item is fully returned; a credit-account rental earns commission once the customer actually pays off that rental's invoice (whether that happens before or after the item comes back). This is deliberate: it rewards staff for following up and closing the loop, not just for booking the rental.
  • Records move through approval (commissions_approve, individually or in bulk) before being marked paid (commissions_pay, also available in bulk). Deleting a record needs its own commissions_delete.
  • A summary view totals commissions owed/paid across employees and time periods.
  • An employee flagged Salesperson (see Employees) only sees their own commission records and their own row of the summary — never other employees' figures.
  • View-only access: giving a Security Group the base commissions permission with none of its sub-permissions checked lets someone see the Dashboard and Records tabs (read-only — no Approve/Pay/Delete buttons appear) without granting any of those actions; unchecking commissions_plans also hides the Plans & Assignments tab entirely, so plan rate structures stay hidden from that group. All of this is enforced server-side too, not just hidden in the UI.

Marketing reports

  • Lead Source Performance — leads, opportunities, and wins per crm_leads.source, with lead→opportunity, opportunity→won, and blended lead→won conversion rates for each source.
  • Customer Segment Report — revenue, discount given, and margin per Customer Category (see Customers), plus a "No Category" row for comparison — shows whether a category's auto-discount is actually driving volume.
  • Loyalty & Cash-Back Engagement — points issued and cash back redeemed over a period, a redemption rate, and the top point earners in that range. There's no historical points ledger in this app (only each customer's running balance), so "points issued" is reconstructed by re-applying the same 0.5-point-per-$1 rate used at checkout over completed sales in range, rather than reading a stored log — noted directly on the report.
  • Discount Card Utilization — cards issued vs. actually used in a sale per card type, plus a list of cardholders who haven't used theirs in the selected range. A card's use isn't tracked by a dedicated link to the transaction — it's detected the same way a Customer Category discount is (matching on the transaction's recorded discount description), which the report explains inline.

Sales reports

  • Sales by Branch & Department — completed sales over a date range for each branch, split into Retail, Rental, Work Orders, and Special Projects, with each branch's total and an All Branches row. A sale counts as Rental / Work Order / Special Project when it's a rental checkout or settlement, a work order assessment/deposit/final payment, or a converted Special Project quote; everything else is Retail. Amounts include tax; refundable rental security deposits are left out.
  • Salesperson Leaderboard — quotes created/converted, win rate, and credited sales per salesperson, keyed off original_employee_id — the same commission-credit field Quotations' Reassign and Commissions already use. Covers every quote type, including Special Projects.
  • Quote Funnel & Cycle Time — how many quotes currently sit in each status, plus the average days spent in Draft, Sent, and Accepted (and, for Special Projects, the extra Draft→Submitted and Submitted→Approved detour). Cycle time is only measured going forward from when this tracking was added — a quote created before then, or one that skipped a stage entirely (e.g. converted straight from Draft), simply isn't counted in that stage's average rather than showing a misleading 0.
  • Special Projects Margin Report — the same report described under Special Projects, linked here too for convenience; only shown to those who already hold special_projects or special_projects_approve.
  • CRM Pipeline / Weighted Forecast — open opportunities (not yet won or lost) grouped by stage, with each stage's total value and probability-weighted forecast value. Defaults to opportunities expected to close in the next 90 days; opportunities with no expected close date are always included regardless of the range.
  • Rental Utilization — bookings, quantity booked, and revenue per rental item (bundled/optional accessories aren't counted separately from their parent item), plus how many units of each are checked out on an active rental right now — sort by revenue to see what's carrying the rental book vs. what's rarely booked.
  • Salesperson Targets — the same Hourly/Daily/Weekly/Monthly target-vs-current figures as the CRM & Sales → Targets tab, laid out as one table (current, target, and % per period per salesperson) instead of progress bars, for printing or exporting. No date range to pick — targets and current sales are always "right now" for each period, same as the Targets tab this reuses.

A running trend list (most recent first) sits underneath the summary tables.
  • Credit Risk / Exposure — every credit-enabled customer currently carrying a balance: how much, what % of their credit limit, whether they're already blocked, and — for anyone not yet blocked — how many days remain before the same auto-block rule Customers already uses (days overdue vs. their payment terms) would block them. A point-in-time snapshot, not date-ranged.
  • Tax Collected — taxable sales and tax actually collected, by day and by branch, plus the effective rate — sized for handing to whoever files sales tax.

Reports & Analytics reports

Sales, inventory, and financial reporting across the business.

  • Sales — totals, trend by day, breakdown by payment method, for any date range and (optionally) a single branch.
  • Top Products and Sales by Category.
  • Inventory report — stock value (cost and retail), and the low-stock list. Warehouses are broken out separately here since they don't generate sales but still hold real stock value.

Branches branches

Your store locations (and warehouses).

Price Tier %

Each branch carries a Price Tier % — a single positive or negative percentage that recalculates every retail item's price at that branch, with no per-product setup. A branch at +10% sells everything 10% above the catalog price; a branch at -15% sells 15% below it. Leave it at 0 for a branch that should just use the flat catalog price.

Where it appliesThe tier recalculates prices at the POS register (the product grid, cart, and checkout total once a branch is selected), Quotations (a quote's line prices follow whichever branch is picked on the quote, re-filling if the branch is changed), and Reports' branch retail-value figures. It does not affect the WooCommerce/online store, which always sells at the flat catalog price. The computed price is floor-clamped at 0 (a very negative tier can't push a price below zero) and rounded to the nearest cent; the untouched catalog price is always what's edited on the product itself — the tier is a multiplier applied on top, never stored per-product.

Needs branches_add / branches_edit / branches_delete.

  • Tax (settings_tax) — default tax rate used where a product doesn't specify its own.
  • Payment (settings_payment) — accepted payment methods, credit terms defaults, layaway deposit percentage and cancellation fee.
  • Integrations (settings_integrations):
    • Email/SMTP — credentials for sending receipts, statements, and notifications by email.
    • Cloudinary — optional cloud image storage for product photos and PO/quote attachments; recommended (and required on Vercel-hosted deployments, where local file storage doesn't persist).
    • WooCommerce — two-way sync of products and orders with a WooCommerce store. Once configured, the app syncs automatically on an interval you set, and you can trigger a manual sync or review the sync log here too. Pulling in an order runs it through the same email/phone/full-name duplicate check used everywhere else customers are created (Customers): a match links the order to that existing customer, otherwise a new one is created from the order's billing info — a guest or first-time WooCommerce order is no longer left with no customer attached, and a repeat buyer under the same billing details never ends up duplicated.
  • Customer Categories — a Categories-style manager (name, discount %, active) for classifying customers as Individual, Company, Contractor, or whatever fits — see Customers for how the discount applies at checkout.
Shipping carriersThe Shipping screen under Warehouse Management lets you record a carrier and tracking number per shipment freeform (UPS, FedEx, USPS, DHL, Local Delivery, Other) — there's no live carrier-rate or tracking-API integration; it's a record-keeping field, not a connected shipping service.
