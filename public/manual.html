Getting Started

What RetailPOS is, how to log in, and how the app is organized.

Logging in

Open the app in a browser and sign in with your username and password. On a brand-new install, the default administrator account is username admin, password 123456 — you'll be forced to change this password on first login.

Security notePasswords are stored and checked in plain text and there is no session timeout beyond closing the browser. Don't reuse a sensitive password for the admin account, and change the default immediately on a new installation.

The sidebar

The left sidebar is grouped into hubs — Sales, Accounts, Warehouse, Purchasing, Dispatch, and Admin — plus Dashboard at the top. Clicking a hub opens a tile grid of the individual screens inside it; which tiles you see depends on your permissions (see Security Groups). This manual link itself opens in a new tab so you can keep it open for reference while you work.

Branches

Everything in the app — stock, sales, drawers, warehouse bins — is scoped to a branch. If your account has multi-branch access and is assigned to more than one branch, you'll see a branch switcher; otherwise you're locked to your assigned default branch. See Branches for how locations are set up.

Permissions

What you can see and do is controlled by the Security Group your account belongs to (Administrator, Manager, Cashier, or a custom group your admin created). If a button or section seems to be missing, it's most likely a permissions issue — ask an administrator to check your group under Security Groups.

Dashboard

The landing page after login — a snapshot of how the business (or your branch) is doing right now.

Everyone sees a dashboard regardless of role. It shows:

  • Today's and this month's sales totals and transaction counts
  • Total active customers
  • A low-stock count badge — also shown as a red notification in the top header everywhere in the app, so it's always visible, not just on this page
  • The five most recent transactions
  • A 7-day sales trend
  • A per-branch breakdown, if you have multi-branch access
Low stockThe low-stock count checks each product's stock at the branch level first, falling back to its overall stock figure only for products that have never been tracked per-branch. Rental items and services are never counted as "low stock" since they don't carry ordinary sale inventory.

Point of Sale pos

Where retail sales actually happen — the cash-register screen.

▶ Training video

Completing a Basic Sale (1:19) — narrated walkthrough with captions.

Layout

POS opens full-screen — the sidebar and top header are hidden for a distraction-free register screen. Use the exit icon at the top-left to leave POS and return to the rest of the app. The ticket table on the left lists items on the current sale; the button grid on the right groups every register action (lookups, discounts, hold/quote, void/cancel, payment) into one place, similar to a dedicated terminal.

Opening a sale

The first thing you'll see on entering POS is a prompt to open a cash drawer — you can skip it, but a drawer session lets the app track cash in/out for reconciliation later (see Cash Drawers). Type or scan an item's SKU/barcode into the item-number box (top right) and press Enter to add it directly, or use Item Lookup on the button grid to browse/search the catalog by name and click items in (retail items only — rentals are managed on their own screen). Click a ticket line to select it, then use Item Zoom (view/edit that product), Void Line, or Quantity on the grid — the same actions are also available inline on the row itself (+/−, VOID, ×). Attach a customer via Cust Lookup to accrue loyalty points, apply store credit, trigger a customer's discount card, or sell on a credit account; Cust Zoom opens that customer's history once one is attached.

Discounts, promotions, and discount cards

Open Ticket Discount on the button grid for the discount/promo entry:

  • Manual discount — a cashier with the pos_discounts permission can apply a cart discount directly.
  • Promo codes — type a code at checkout; it's validated against active date windows, usage limits, and minimum-purchase rules before it's applied.
  • Auto-applied promotions — any active, code-less promotion that matches the cart is offered automatically (see Promotions).
  • Discount cards — attaching a customer who carries an active discount card applies their flat percentage automatically, and it always takes priority over a promo code — attaching such a customer clears any promo already on the cart (see Discount Cards).

Line-item discounts

The % button on a cart row (needs pos_discounts) opens a per-line discount, separate from the whole-ticket Ticket Discount above. An item is only eligible if it isn't already covered by an active promotion (no stacking a manual discount on a promo) and its brand isn't on the store's discount-exclusion list (Settings → POS Line-Item Discounts) — otherwise the button explains why and no discount is offered.

A discount up to the store's configured Maximum Line Item Discount % (same settings card, 0 = unlimited) applies immediately. Going over that limit prompts for a manager PIN — an employee whose security group has pos_discount_override — the same on-the-spot override pattern as Void Line; entering it records who authorized the discount right on the line. Every check (eligibility and the limit/override) is re-verified server-side at checkout too, so it can't be bypassed by tampering with the cart in the browser.

Holding an order

Use Hold (needs pos_hold) to park a cart without charging it or touching stock — useful when a customer steps away or a quote is being converted. Use Hold Recall to finish it later from the held-orders list, or delete the hold entirely to abandon it.

Converting the current ticket to a Quote or Layaway

Create Quote and Create Layaway on the grid open the normal quotation/layaway tools (see Quotations), pre-filled from whatever's currently on the ticket — the attached customer, branch, and line items — so you don't have to re-enter them. Both forms stay fully editable before you save, so nothing is locked in by the prefill.

  • Quote: any item with variations (size/color/etc.) is added as a described custom line rather than a catalog line, since quotes don't track which specific variant was intended — the variant name is written into the description so it isn't lost.
  • Layaway: only items that are actually layaway-eligible can go on a layaway plan (see Inventory's services/layaway product settings) — any ticket item that isn't gets left out, with a warning naming how many were skipped, so you can add them to the layaway manually if needed. Creating the layaway clears the POS ticket, since the deposit is collected as part of that flow (needs layaway_create); the resulting plan then follows the normal layaway lifecycle in Layaway — collect installments, pick up on completion — with no difference from one created directly there.

Quote Recall on the grid opens the same quotation lookup tools without leaving the register screen.

Payment methods

Open Payment on the button grid to choose a tender — Cash, Credit Card, Debit Card, Check, Gift Card, and Direct Deposit are all settled immediately. Pay On Acct charges straight to a customer's credit account, which requires the account to be in good standing — a blocked account (overdue past its payment terms) is rejected at checkout with a clear error rather than silently failing.

If Alternate Currencies are configured (Settings), the Cash tender gets a Payment Currency picker — pick any of the store's base or alternate currencies and enter the amount actually handed over in that currency; it converts to the ticket's base currency automatically. Once there's change due, a separate Give Change In picker appears, independent of what currency was tendered — a customer can pay in USD and still get change back in the base currency, or vice versa, whatever the drawer actually has on hand. It defaults to matching the payment currency the first time it appears, but can be switched freely. The receipt and printed invoice always show change in whichever currency was chosen there; the transaction's own recorded amounts (for accounting/drawer reconciliation) stay in the base currency regardless.

Split Tender lets one sale be paid across two or more of the immediate-settlement methods above — for example part Cash and part Credit Card. Add a row per method, enter the exact amount each should cover, and add a reference/approval number where that method normally needs one (Credit Card, Debit Card, Gift Card, Direct Deposit); the rows must add up to the ticket total exactly before Confirm Payment is enabled. Pay On Acct can't be one of the split legs — a charge-account sale stays its own single-method tender. Since each leg is settled for its exact amount, there's no change-back on a split sale's cash leg the way there is on a plain Cash sale — if change is owed, apply a smaller amount to Cash and hand back the difference yourself. If Alternate Currencies are configured (see Settings), a Cash row gets its own currency picker just like the plain Cash tender does — enter the amount actually handed over in that currency and it converts to the ticket's base currency automatically. The receipt, transaction detail, and drawer reconciliation all show a split sale's tender legs itemized rather than as one payment method.

Tax exemption

Tax is normally waived automatically for a customer whose profile has the tax-exempt flag set. For a one-off exemption that shouldn't require editing that customer's record — a walk-in government or non-profit purchase, for instance — use Tax Exempt on the button grid to mark just the current ticket, optionally recording an exemption number. This overrides the customer's saved flag for this sale only; the ticket totals update immediately, and the transaction/receipt are labeled tax-exempt the same way a profile-driven exemption would be.

Available credit (credit notes)

When a customer has an outstanding credit note — issued from a return (see Transactions & Returns) or a rental deposit (see Warehouse Management) — attaching them to a ticket shows an Available Credit bar with the balance the store owes them. Apply uses as much of it as the current ticket total needs (capped at whichever is smaller), reducing what's still owed by exactly the amount applied once the sale completes. If the applied credit brings the ticket to $0, opening Payment skips tender selection entirely and offers a single Complete Sale button instead — no cash/card/other tender needs to be picked, and the transaction is recorded as paid by Available Credit rather than as a $0 cash sale.

Cash back

If the attached customer carries an active Cash Back Card and has enough loyalty points accrued to clear its rules, a Cash Back bar appears the same way the Available Credit bar does. Apply uses as much of the available cash back as the ticket needs, and it stacks with Available Credit if both are active on the same customer — the ticket total is reduced by both. If the two together bring the ticket to $0, Payment again skips tender selection and offers a single Complete Sale button. Redeeming deducts the corresponding loyalty points proportionally (not the whole accrued chunk if only part of it was applied) and resets that card's redemption cooldown, if it has one.

Voids, cancels, and refunds

Voiding a line item mid-sale (Void Line, or the row's own VOID button) needs pos_void_items and an authorizing PIN. Cancel Ticket clears the whole sale in progress. Refunding or returning items on an already-completed sale is handled from Transactions — the Returns button on the grid jumps straight there — and needs transactions_returns/pos_refunds.

Receipts and labels

After checkout you can print a standard receipt, or re-open the last completed sale's receipt with Reprint Ticket (scoped to the current register session). For shippable orders, print a pick list / shipping label from the Shipping screen — see Warehouse Management.

Cash Drawers drawers

Tracking physical cash in and out of the register across a shift.

  • Sessions — open a drawer at the start of a shift with a starting float, close it at the end. Only staff with drawers_open/drawers_close can do so, and reconciling a closed session (counting the actual cash against what the system expects) needs drawers_manage.
  • Reconciliation uses your store's configured currency denominations (bill and coin values) so you count physical notes, not just type in a total — differences (over/short) are recorded per session.
  • Manage Drawers tab — set up which physical registers/drawers exist per branch, independent of who's using them on a given day.

Transactions & Returns transactions

The full history of completed (and held) sales, plus the returns workflow.

  • Search/filter by date, customer, status, branch, payment method, or transaction number.
  • Returns (needs transactions_returns) — process a full or partial return against a completed sale; stock and any applicable fees are adjusted automatically.
  • Exporting transaction history needs transactions_export.
  • Online Orders — sales that came in through the WooCommerce integration (or any API-key-authenticated channel) appear here tagged as online, with their own fulfillment status you can advance toward shipping.
Held ordersA hold created by converting an accepted quotation is linked back to that quote — finishing the sale marks the quote "converted"; deleting the hold instead reverts the quote back to "accepted" so it isn't left stranded.

Quotations quotations

Formal price quotes for a customer, for either retail goods or a rental, that can later become a real sale.

Two kinds of quote

Retail quotes price from the current catalog and can mix real products with free-typed custom line items (for things not yet in your catalog). Rental quotes are priced as a non-binding estimate using the item's daily/weekly/monthly rate over the requested date range, and can include delivery, pickup, and operator add-ons. Switching Quote Type to Rental also narrows the Customer picker to customers already flagged Rental Customer on their profile (see Customers) — the same eligibility a customer needs before a rental agreement can be created directly.

Status flow

  1. Draft — still being built; can be freely edited.
  2. Sent — shared with the customer.
  3. Accepted — the customer has committed. For retail quotes, this is the moment anything not currently in stock at the quote's branch is automatically handled: stock available at another branch triggers an automatic branch transfer, and anything no branch can cover (or a custom item never in the catalog) is bundled into a single purchase request for Purchasing to action.
  4. Converted — the quote becomes real: a retail quote becomes a held order a cashier finalizes at the POS; a rental quote becomes a rental agreement (after re-checking stock and the customer's rental eligibility, since time may have passed since the quote was drafted).

A quote can also be declined instead of accepted. Needs quotations_create to build one, quotations_approve to move it through status changes, and quotations_convert to finalize it into a sale or agreement.

Per-line discount

Each retail line — a real product or a free-typed custom item — has its own optional Discount field, separate from the whole-quote Discount ($) field. It's a record of how much was knocked off that specific line, shown next to the item wherever the line shows up; it doesn't itself change the price you type in — enter the already-discounted price there. If the line isn't in stock and gets bundled into a Purchase Request (or later a Purchase Order) at Accept, its discount comes along too, shown as a small tag on that line — so whoever approves the PR or PO can see the item is backing a discounted sale before signing off.

Layaway layaway

Reserve merchandise for a customer against a schedule of installment payments.

  • Only products explicitly marked layaway-eligible can go on a plan.
  • Stock is reserved immediately when the plan is created, not when it's paid off — so it won't get sold to someone else in the meantime.
  • A minimum deposit (a store-wide percentage) is required up front; the customer can choose weekly, biweekly, or monthly installments after that.
  • Every deposit and installment is recorded as a real transaction, so it shows up in normal cash-drawer and sales reporting.
  • Paying off the full balance auto-completes the plan and awards loyalty points.
  • Cancelling a plan restores the reserved stock and refunds whatever the customer already paid, minus a configurable cancellation fee (percentage or flat) that's forfeited.

Needs layaway_create to start a plan, layaway_payments to record installments, layaway_cancel to cancel one.

Rentals rentals

Rent out equipment instead of selling it, with its own catalog, compliance requirements, and checkout/return workflow.

Rental Items

Managed separately from retail Inventory. Each item has a daily rate, a classification (Tool or Equipment), a model number and size, and can have mandatory or optional accessories attached (needs rentals_manage_items).

Tool pricing tiersTools get automatic volume discounts off the daily rate instead of a manually-set weekly/monthly catalog price: renting 7-28 days bills the whole rental at 85% of the daily rate per day, and renting more than 28 days bills the whole rental at 75% of that (i.e. 63.75% of the original daily rate) per day. The Weekly/Monthly Rate fields on the item form are only used for Equipment items, which still bill in fixed week/month blocks at their own configured rates plus an hourly rate for partial days.

Selling a rental item outright

Checking "Also allow selling or quoting this item outright, like a retail item" on the item form (with a required Selling Price) makes it show up in POS's product grid and a Retail Quotation's item picker too — tagged RENTAL ITEM / [Rental] so staff can tell it apart from ordinary stock — in addition to still being rentable. It's a genuine outright sale: the unit sold is deducted from stock for good, the same as any retail item, and doesn't come back the way a returned rental does. Only units not currently out on an active rental are offered for sale — the item's stock figure is the total number owned, so whatever's checked out to a rental customer is excluded from what POS/Quotations will sell. The For Sale · [price] tag on the Rental Items list shows which items this is turned on for.

Moving a rental item between locations

A rental item lives at a single branch at a time (its Branch / Location on the item form) — only staff at that branch, or with multi-branch access, can see and rent it. There are two ways to change that:

  • Edit the item directly — changing the Branch / Location dropdown moves its stock to the new branch instantly, with no transfer record. Fine for an item that's never been rented, but blocked (with an error naming how many units and telling you to use Branch Transfers instead) whenever units of it are still out on an active rental from its current branch — reassigning it out from under an active loan would leave that rental's return pointing at a branch the item no longer shows as belonging to.
  • Transfer (needs transfers) — the Transfer button on the item's row in Rental Items jumps into Branch Transfers with the item and its current branch pre-filled, going through the same pickup/receive audit trail retail stock uses. Only units actually free right now can be moved — a rental item's stock figure is the total number owned, so whatever's currently checked out to a customer from that branch is subtracted from what's offered for transfer, both in the item picker and if attempted anyway.

Customer eligibility

A customer can't start a new rental until their profile has an ID (type, number, and a scanned copy), a proof of address, and an emergency reference on file. This is enforced at both the direct rental-hold flow and at converting a rental quote into an agreement.

Rental Dispatch — the agreement lifecycle

  1. Create — pick items and a due date; availability is checked for that window.
  2. Checkout (needs rentals_checkout) — finalizes pricing and takes the deposit. If billed to the customer's charge account (Charge Account (Credit) payment method), a customer PO number and a copy of the PO document are required before the checkout can complete — the PO is what ties the account charge back to the customer's own paperwork.
  3. Issue (needs rentals_issue) — records the physical handoff to the customer; this can happen on a different date than checkout, and can include delivery (with driver and cost) or a customer pickup, plus an optional equipment operator. Required to complete this step: a security employee verifying the item leaving and signing to confirm it, plus the customer's own signature (drawn on-screen) confirming receipt — two separate signatures, each viewable individually from the agreement's detail view.
  4. Return (needs rentals_returns) — processes the item coming back, assessing any late fees or damage fees against the deposit. Required to complete this step: a security employee signing the item back in — always. A driver select, confirming who collected the item, only appears when the agreement has pickup as part of it; a walk-in return with no pickup shows no driver field at all. If the driver already confirmed the pickup in the field (see below), a green banner replaces the signature pad — no redundant signature is collected here. A rental can't be returned while it's paused (see below) — resume it first.

An agreement can also be cancelled with a reason on file instead of following through to return.

Assigning & confirming a pickup driverUnlike delivery (assigned as part of Issue), a pickup driver can be assigned ahead of the actual pickup — click Assign next to "Not yet assigned" in the Pickup column (Rental Dispatch's Deliveries tab, the Rentals list, or the agreement detail view; needs rentals_issue). That driver then sees the job under Pickups Needed on their own My Deliveries dashboard and, if their security group has rentals_confirm_pickup, taps Confirm Pickup once they've actually collected the item — the customer enters their name and signs on the driver's device right there, no cashier involved. This only records that the item was physically collected; it doesn't touch billing. Process Return still finalizes the financial settlement afterward (still gated on rentals_returns, independently of confirm-pickup access), whenever it's convenient for a cashier to do it — it just skips asking for another driver signature since the field confirmation already has one.
Confirm-pickup access is its own permissionrentals_confirm_pickup is separate from rentals_returns on purpose — a security group can be set up so a driver sees and confirms their assigned pickups (the signature capture) without ever being able to open Process Return. Without this permission, the job still shows on the driver's My Deliveries dashboard (so they know what's expected of them) but the Confirm Pickup button itself is hidden, and the backend rejects the action if called directly. Being flagged Driver on an employee's profile only controls whether they land on My Deliveries and which jobs are assigned to them — it does not by itself grant any action; that's the security group's job.
Security employeesOnly employees flagged Security on their profile (Employees → edit, alongside the existing Driver/Operator checkboxes) appear in the Issue/Return security dropdowns. Since these checkpoints are required, at least one employee needs that flag set before any rental can be issued or returned — and at both Issue and Return, that employee has to draw their own signature on-screen (not just pick their name) to confirm. The rentals list shows a "✓ Issue" / "✓ Return" badge once each side's checkpoints — including the guard's signature — are on file; a dash means either that stage hasn't happened yet or (for rentals issued/returned before this existed) there's no record.
A balance owed at return goes to the register, not the guardThe item itself comes back into stock the moment security signs it in, regardless of any money owed — but if the settlement (damage fees plus any duration adjustment, less the deposit) comes out positive on a non-credit rental, the return completes with no transaction charged yet rather than assuming whoever processed the return collected it in cash. The agreement sits fully Returned and shows up in the POS Hold Recall list labeled RENTAL BALANCE DUE with the amount owed, the same "pay at the register" pattern Work Orders use for their final balance. A cashier picks it up from there, sees the full breakdown (damage fees, duration adjustment, tax, less the deposit), and takes payment — that's what actually creates the settlement transaction, tied to their own drawer session, with a receipt (including the letter-size invoice) shown immediately after. A return that nets a refund instead (deposit exceeds what's owed) still settles immediately at the point of return — there's no cash to collect from the customer, so there's nothing to hold.

Pausing a rental

An issued (active) rental can be paused for Maintenance, Replacement of Item, or Other — for when the item has to come back to the shop mid-rental without that counting as a return. Pausing stops the billing clock: the paused time is excluded from the rental fee charged at return, and the due date is automatically pushed out by however long the pause lasted (rounded up to the nearest whole day) once it's resumed.

Pause requires manager overrideAnyone who can see the agreement can open Pause Rental, but starting the pause requires a manager/admin PIN on the spot (needs rentals_pause) — same override pattern as voiding a POS cart line — plus a reason and notes explaining what's happening. Resuming doesn't need a PIN. While paused, the agreement shows a Paused status on the list (with the current pause's start time) and a full Pause History — reason, notes, start/stop times, who paused/authorized/resumed it, and the due-date extension — is kept on the agreement's detail view and on its printed invoice.

Missed pickups

Rental Dispatch's Pickup Reminders tab highlights every active, pickup-required rental approaching its due date/time — not just the day, the actual hour, combining the due date with the time of day the rental was checked out at. Rows are bucketed and color-coded: amber for due within 24 hours, red for due within 8 hours. It's a live view, not a sent notification — nothing is emailed or logged just for appearing on this list.

If the due date/time passes and the item still hasn't come back, the system automatically pauses the agreement (reason Missed Pickup) — no manager PIN, since there's no one present to enter one for a system action. The agreement's detail view then shows a highlighted callout in place of the normal pause banner, with three actions:

  • Email Customer — sends a follow-up email (editable message, defaults to a standard "we couldn't complete your pickup" note) and logs that the attempt was made.
  • Log Call — for when staff called instead; just records the outcome, no email sent.
  • Record Customer Decision — once staff has actually reached the customer, this is where their decision gets documented: Continue (pick a new pickup date; the pause ends and the due date updates to it) or Stop (the pause ends with no extension — the agreement drops back into the normal Active/Overdue view, unblocked for Process Return whenever the item actually comes back). Either way an optional confirmation email can go out to the customer summarizing what was agreed.
The customer's decision is staff-attested, not self-serviceThere's no reply-parsing or click-a-link infrastructure in this app — recording "the customer confirmed continue/stop" means a staff member is vouching for a conversation that happened by phone or email, the same way every other rental checkpoint in this app (guard/customer signatures) is a human physically confirming something, not a customer-facing portal. The confirmation, whoever logged it, and when, are all kept on that specific missed-pickup pause record — visible in Pause History on the agreement.

Driver & Guard dashboards

An employee flagged Driver or Security (Employees → edit) lands on a purpose-built dashboard instead of the regular Dashboard when they log in — neither role needs sales totals or low-stock counts, just their own rental checkpoint queue.

  • My Deliveries (Driver) — Deliveries Needed (paid, awaiting issue, delivery required, not yet dispatched — a shared pool any driver can act on, since delivery has no pre-assignment step), Pickups Needed (only pickups a dispatcher has assigned to this driver and not yet confirmed, soonest due date first, each with a Confirm Pickup action), and My Recent Activity (deliveries/pickups this employee has personally completed).
  • My Verifications (Security) — Awaiting Security Check (paid, awaiting issue, no security sign-off yet), Expected Returns (all active rentals, soonest due date first, so a guard knows what to expect at the gate), and My Recent Verifications (issue/return sign-offs this employee has personally recorded).

Deliveries and security checks are still a shared pool anyone with the flag can act on — they're recorded at the moment Issue or Return actually happens, with no assignment ahead of time. Pickups are the one exception (see the callout above): a dispatcher assigns a specific driver first, so that driver's dashboard only shows their own jobs, not everyone's. Clicking View on any row opens the same agreement detail used everywhere else in Rentals. An employee flagged as both Driver and Security lands on My Deliveries by default but can still reach My Verifications from the sidebar.

Rental Deposits & Credit Notes

Tracks deposits collected and refunded per agreement, and lets staff issue a credit note when a partial or adjusted refund is warranted instead of a straight deposit return. Summary cards at the top break down every deposit currently in the system: how much is still Held on active/awaiting-issue rentals, how much has been refunded through each channel (Cash, Bank Transfer, Original Card), how much went out as Store Credit, how much was Applied to Account (charge-account rentals — automatic, no manual step), and how much is Awaiting Method — a refund is due but nobody has recorded how it was actually paid out yet.

When a return leaves a refund due (needs rentals_returns), each row offers two actions: Issue Credit Note (unchanged — puts the refund on the customer's account as store credit instead of paying it out), or Record Refund — logs which of Cash, Bank Transfer, or Refund to Original Card was actually used, plus an optional reference number (transfer ref, approval code) and notes. This is bookkeeping only: the money itself is expected to move outside the app (or already has) the moment the return posts a refund-type settlement — this step just records which channel it went through, for the summary cards above and for the policy check below.

The refund method dropdown is pre-selected per policy, not lockedBased on the deposit's original payment method, amount, and age, the recorded refund method defaults to: Cash/Debit deposits of J$10,000 or less → Cash; over J$10,000 → Bank Transfer. Credit card deposits refunded within 90 days of the original charge → back to the original card; past 90 days → Bank Transfer. Direct Deposit, Bank Transfer, and Cheque originals → Bank Transfer only. The policy note shown in the modal explains which rule applied — staff can still pick a different method for a real exception, it just won't carry that note.

Agreements returned before this tracking existed show Awaiting Method until someone goes back and records how that refund was actually handled — a one-time backfill pass, not something the app can infer on its own.

Work Orders work_orders

A ticketing system for service/repair intake — a customer drops off an item, staff open a Work Order (WO) against it, and it moves through assessment, deposit, and repair before the customer is notified and the item is picked up.

Lifecycle

  1. Intake (needs wo_intake) — pick the customer, optionally the branch, an item/tag label, and a description of what's being serviced. A label prints automatically showing the WO number, ready to attach to the item. The description stays editable at any point before completion — changing it is logged in the status history, not silently overwritten.
  2. Assessment Fee — a single non-refundable fee, set storewide in Settings → Work Orders, is collected before the item is assessed. Status moves to Assessed.
  3. Estimate (needs wo_assess) — labor and consumables (oil, grease, fuel, etc.) are entered as estimate dollar amounts, along with a required deposit amount. Status moves to Pending Deposit.
  4. Deposit — once collected, status moves to In Progress — parts assignment, technician work, and time tracking (a later phase) happen from here.
Fees are collected at the register only — never from the Work Orders screenThe assessment fee and the deposit are cash-handling steps, kept separate from whoever did the assessment. Until it's paid, the WO's own status (intake / pending_deposit) is an implicit "on hold" state: it shows up in the POS Hold Recall list alongside held sales and pending rentals, labeled WORK ORDER with the fee amount due, so a cashier can pick it up and process payment from the register — the same pattern a rental checkout's "pending" list uses. Opening the WO itself never shows a way to take the payment directly, no matter who's looking at it (including Administrators/Managers) — it just shows an "On hold — awaiting cashier" badge, pointing staff to the register instead.

Status history

Every transition — intake, fee paid, estimate entered, deposit paid, cancelled, plus free-form updates once a WO is In Progress, Awaiting Parts, or Awaiting Sign-Off — is logged with who did it and an optional comment. This is the running activity feed shown on the WO's detail view, so a customer-facing service rep can see exactly what's happened without digging through separate screens.

From Add Status Update, a WO that's In Progress, Awaiting Parts, or Awaiting Sign-Off can be freely moved between those three (e.g. pause it to Awaiting Parts while waiting on a backordered part, then move it back to In Progress once the part arrives), or set to Not Worth Fixing — a terminal outcome for when the item turns out not to be repairable or economical to fix once work has started. Not Worth Fixing locks the WO the same way Cancelled does: no further parts, description, or status edits.

Pickup window

Every WO gets a 30-day pickup-due date, counted from when it was opened (not from completion) — shown as its own badge on the WO list and detail view, independent of lifecycle status. A repair that takes longer than 30 days can show as approaching or past its pickup window before it's even finished; this is intentional, not a bug.

Cancelling

A WO can be cancelled (with a required reason) any time before the deposit is paid. Once it's In Progress or further along, it can no longer be cancelled — the deposit has been collected and work has started.

Parts

Parts (needs wo_assign_parts) can be identified and assigned from the retail catalog as soon as the WO is opened — no need to wait for the deposit, so a technician can pull or order parts while the estimate and deposit are still being sorted out with the customer. Editing stays open through every pre-completion status (Intake, Assessed, Pending Deposit, In Progress, Awaiting Parts, Awaiting Sign-Off); it locks once the WO reaches Complete, Cancelled, or Not Worth Fixing. Each "+ Add Part" line picks one of three types:

  • Catalog Product (in stock or elsewhere) — pick this for anything that's actually in the product catalog, even if this branch is out of stock. Sourcing works exactly like a retail quotation: if the WO's own branch has enough stock, the part is simply reserved from there; if not, the shortfall can be split across other branches (which auto-creates a real branch transfer) or left unassigned (which auto-creates a Purchase Request) — this happens automatically, there's nothing else to pick for "out of stock."
  • Not In Our Catalog — Order It — a free-text description and price for a part that isn't in the catalog at all yet, the same "Q" line a retail quotation uses for a custom item. It carries no branch-availability check; instead it's automatically flagged straight into the WO's Purchase Request on Confirm Parts, tagged Q with a link to that PR. Once the resulting Purchase Order is received, linking it to a product (new or existing, from Purchasing's "+ Add to Inventory" on the unmatched line) flips the WO's line back over to that real product automatically — closing the loop the same way it does for a quotation's Q items. A brand-new product created this way is tagged non-inventory, same as a quote's.
  • Customer Brought Their Own — the customer supplied the part themselves. It's tracked on the WO (so the technician knows to use it) but is never sourced, never sent to Purchasing, and never charged — quantity and a description only, tagged Customer Supplied. Don't use this for a part the shop still needs to order — that's the "Not In Our Catalog" option above.

Each line shows a one-line hint under it explaining exactly what picking that type will do — read it before saving if you're not sure which one applies.

Save Parts records what's needed; Confirm Parts is the separate, explicit step that actually creates those transfers/purchase requests — the same "confirm what was drafted" split a quotation's Accept step uses. Each part line shows a live status badge (sourced from branch / transfer pending / PR pending / PO received / Q / Customer Supplied) that updates the same way a quotation's does once its linked purchase order comes in.

A catalog part that's pulled for a job but ends up not being installed — or one removed from the customer's machine and still resalable — can be sent back with Return on its line (any pre-completion status, needs wo_assign_parts). It asks how many to return and at what discounted resale price, then restocks that quantity under a distinct "(Used)" sibling product (its own SKU, price, and stock, tagged Used in Inventory) rather than mixing it back into the original product's new-condition stock — created automatically the first time a part of that product is returned, and topped up (at the latest asking price) on later returns. The original line keeps its assigned quantity for the record but shows how many were returned, and its charge on the work order drops to cover only what's left.

Tasks & time tracking

Once a WO is In Progress (needs wo_technician), it can be broken into tasks — a description, an allotted time in minutes, and an assigned technician. Each task has a Clock In / Clock Out button: clocking in starts a timer (and flips the task to In Progress); clocking out stops it. Actual time is the sum of every clock-in/clock-out pair on that task, so a technician can pause and resume across a break without losing what's already been logged. Actual time is shown in red once it exceeds the allotted minutes.

One timer per technicianA technician can only be clocked into one task at a time, anywhere in the system — clocking into a second task while still clocked into another is blocked with a message telling them to clock out of the first one first. This is what keeps the time data trustworthy enough to actually evaluate whether tasks are completed on time.

The ⏱ Active Tasks button on the Work Orders list header opens a live popup (refreshing every 15 seconds) showing every technician currently clocked in, which task, which WO, and elapsed vs. allotted time — a quick way for a supervisor to see who's working on what without opening each WO individually.

Skills & scheduling

A managed skill catalog ("Manage Skills" on the Work Orders list) lets you define what a technician can do (e.g. "Engine Repair", "Electrical"). Skills are assigned to technicians from their employee edit form, and can optionally be tagged as required on a task — the schedule board shows which skills each technician has next to unassigned tasks so it's obvious who's a fit.

The 📅 Schedule button opens a day-by-day board: pick a date, see every unscheduled task (with its required skills, if any) on one side, and everyone already assigned to that day on the other. Assigning a technician to a task for a given day doesn't lock anything — it's a planning aid, not a hard reservation, and can be removed and reassigned freely.

Sign-off, notification & pickup

Supervisor Sign-Off (needs wo_signoff) is only available once every task on the work order is marked Complete — signing off moves the WO to Complete and records who signed off and when.

Notify Customer then offers two ways to reach them: Send Email Notification (only shown if the customer has an email on file) sends a "ready for pickup" email and records it, or Mark as Called logs that they were reached by phone without sending anything. Either way, the WO moves to Awaiting Pickup.

Once the customer arrives, Process Final Payment is what closes the work order out — there's no separate "Mark Picked Up" step anymore. It's the same cashier-only, register-collected pattern as the assessment fee and deposit: the WO screen itself only ever shows an "On hold — awaiting cashier" badge, never a way to take the payment directly, and the WO appears in the POS Hold Recall list labeled WORK ORDER with the balance due, so a cashier picks it up and processes it from the register. The modal shows the full breakdown — estimate (labor + consumables) plus parts, less the deposit already paid — and charges exactly that balance. If the deposit already covers the final cost, the balance is JMD 0 and the button simply reads Complete Pickup with no payment method to pick — no transaction is created, but the WO still only advances to Picked Up through this one action.

The pickup-due badge keeps running the whole timeRemember the 30-day pickup window counts from when the WO was opened, not from completion — a work order can already show "Pickup overdue" on the Awaiting Pickup list the moment it lands there, if the repair itself took a while. That's expected, not a bug — it's meant to flag exactly that situation.

Promotions promotions

Cart- or catalog-wide discounts, either automatic or code-triggered.

  • A promotion is a percentage or fixed discount, optionally scoped to specific products or categories, with an optional date window and minimum purchase.
  • A product or category can only belong to one active promotion at a time — the system blocks adding it to a second one while the first is still active, so promotions never silently stack on the same item.
  • A promotion can have one or more codes, each with its own usage limit — retiring one code doesn't kill the whole promotion.
  • At checkout, promotions with no code attached are offered automatically; the best (largest discount) one is suggested first if more than one qualifies.
  • Start Time / End Time (optional) — narrows a promotion to a window of the day, e.g. a 2:00 PM-4:00 PM happy hour. Left blank, the promotion runs all day on any date it's otherwise eligible.
  • Recurrence — None (the default) runs the promotion once: Start Time/End Time apply only to the Start Date and End Date themselves, so a one-time window can still span several full days in between, e.g. a weekend sale from Friday 6:00 PM to Sunday 11:59 PM. Daily repeats the time window every day. Weekly repeats it on the day(s) of the week checked in the form (defaults to the Start Date's own weekday if none are checked). Monthly/Yearly repeat on the Start Date's day-of-month / month-and-day (clamped to the last day of a shorter month, e.g. a 31st-anchored monthly promo runs on the 28th/29th in February). Every recurrence is still bounded by the optional Start Date/End Date range.
  • Applies To → Specific Brands — scopes the promotion to one or more brands (set on each product — see Inventory). Pick which brands from the promotion's own Brands button on the list, the same way Items assigns products/categories. A brand can only be the inclusion set of one active promotion at a time, same exclusivity rule as products/categories.
  • Brand exclusion — independent of Applies To, the same Brands button can exclude a brand from a promotion no matter what it applies to (all items, a category, specific products, or another brand) — e.g. "15% off everything except Brand X." Multiple promotions can each exclude the same brand with no conflict, since exclusion never stacks a second discount on top.

Needs promotions_create to build a promotion, promotions_codes to manage its codes.

Discount Cards discount-cards

A simple flat percentage tied to a customer, presented as a physical card.

Set up card types here (a name plus a flat discount percentage), then assign a type and card number to a customer from their profile. There's no date window, no code, no product scoping — attaching a card-carrying customer to a cart just applies the discount automatically, and it always overrides a promo code rather than combining with one. A card type can't be deleted while any customer still references it — deactivate it instead to stop it applying without breaking customer history.

Cash Back Cards cash-back-cards

A loyalty-points-based rewards program, redeemable as cash off a future sale rather than a point-of-sale discount.

Set up card types here, each defining how a customer's existing loyalty points (earned automatically on every sale, see Customers) convert into redeemable cash back:

  • Points Threshold / Reward Amount — the conversion rate, expressed as whole chunks rather than a continuous rate: e.g. a threshold of 500 and a reward of $5 means every full 500 points accrued is worth $5, so 1,200 points is worth $10 (two full chunks), not $12.
  • Minimum Redeem Amount — optional; the customer's accrued cash back must reach at least this dollar amount before any of it can be applied. Leave at 0 to disable this gate.
  • Redemption Cooldown (days) — optional; once a customer redeems cash back, they can't redeem again until this many days have passed since that redemption. Leave at 0 to disable this gate. This only applies between redemptions — a customer's very first redemption only needs to clear the minimum amount gate, if set.

Both gates are independently optional, but when set, both must be satisfied at the same time before the Cash Back bar appears at checkout — see Point of Sale. Assign a type and an optional card number to a customer from their profile, the same way a discount card is assigned. A card type can't be deleted while any customer still references it — deactivate it instead to stop it applying without breaking customer history.

Inventory inventory

The retail product catalog and stock levels — rental items and services live in their own sections.

Products

Each product has a SKU, barcode, price, cost, tax rate, category, supplier, an optional free-text Brand, and a minimum-stock threshold used for low-stock alerts. Products can carry a photo (uploaded per product) and be organized by category. Brand is also what Promotions uses for brand-scoped and brand-excluded discounts.

Variations

For products that come in options (Size, Color, etc.), define variation types on the product, then add individual variants — each with its own SKU, price, and stock level. A sale against a specific variant deducts that variant's stock, not the parent product's.

Stock — two numbers to know about

Every product has a single overall stock level, and separately a per-branch stock level once it's been touched by a branch-specific action (a sale, a received PO, a transfer, etc.). Most day-to-day stock changes update both automatically. If you ever see a stock number that looks "off" compared to what a branch report shows, this is usually why — see the note under Reports.

Adjusting stock and stock movements

Manual stock adjustments (needs inventory_adjust) and every automatic change (sales, receiving, transfers, cycle-count corrections) are logged as stock movements you can review per product, so you can always trace why a number changed.

Merging duplicate SKUs

Merge on a product's row (needs inventory_edit) consolidates it into another product you pick from a searchable list: the surviving product's stock (overall and per-branch) absorbs the duplicate's, and every sale, purchase order, quotation, work order, transfer, cycle count, bin assignment, and other record that named the duplicate is repointed to the survivor — so reports and history read as one SKU going forward. The duplicate itself is never deleted; it's deactivated and tagged Merged → [surviving SKU] on the inventory list, so anything that still shows its old name or SKU (a printed receipt, an old invoice) still traces back to a real record. This can't be undone from the UI — it would take a database-level fix — so double-check the target before confirming. Rental items and anything with active variations can't be merged (they don't offer the button); a variation-level duplicate should be cleaned up on the product's own Variations tab instead.

CSV import/export

Bulk-manage the catalog with a CSV template you can download, edit, and re-import — useful for large catalog updates or migrating from another system. A separate template/import exists specifically for rental items.

Quote-sourced items — the "Non-Inventory Items" tab

When a quotation includes a "Q" item — something quoted for a customer that isn't in the catalog yet — and its Purchase Order is received and linked to a new product (see Purchasing), that product is stocked and tracked normally, but it was bought to fulfill one specific quote, not for general resale. It's automatically kept out of the main product table above and listed instead under the Non-Inventory Items tab on this page.

Items there are still fully searchable and selectable when building a new quotation or purchase request, so ordering more of the same thing later works exactly like normal. Once you're ready to stock it for everyone — not just the customer it was originally quoted to — click Add to Warehouse Inventory on its row; it immediately moves into the main Inventory list as-is.

Not the same as ServicesThis is a different idea from the Services & Non-Inventory Items module below. A service/non-inventory-flagged product has stock tracking turned off permanently. An item on this "Non-Inventory Items" tab is a completely normal, stock-tracked product — it's just hidden from the main list until someone promotes it.

Permissions

inventory_add to create products, inventory_edit to change them, inventory_delete to remove them, inventory_adjust for manual stock corrections.

Services & Non-Inventory Items services

Things you sell that aren't physical stock — labor, installation, warranties, etc.

These are ordinary products under the hood (same catalog, same /products data), just flagged as a service or non-inventory item, which turns off stock tracking for them entirely. They won't ever show up in low-stock alerts or stock reports, and selling one never touches a stock number.

Purchasing purchasing purchase_requests

Getting new stock from suppliers, from an internal request through to receiving it.

Purchase Requests (PR) — the ask

Staff request items either for resale or for internal use (consumables that won't track stock). A request line can point at an existing catalog product, or just be typed in by name with an optional source URL — handy for something not in the catalog yet. Requests flow draft → submitted → approved/rejected (needs pr_create / pr_approve), and an approved request converts 1:1 into a Purchase Order (pr_convert). A line auto-created from an accepted quotation carries that quote line's discount forward as a small tag, visible here and again on the resulting PO — see Quotations' Per-line discount note.

Purchase Orders (PO) — the supplier-facing order

  1. Draft — built directly, from a converted PR, or sent back here by a rejection. Fully editable: items (qty/cost), supplier, branch, dates, and notes can all be changed while a PO is in Draft.
  2. Sent — formally submitted for approval.
  3. Approved or Rejected (needs purchasing_approve) — the decision on a submitted PO.
  4. Receiving (needs purchasing_receive) — mark items as arrived; this can happen over multiple partial deliveries. Receiving updates stock (both overall and at the PO's branch) and refreshes the product's cost to the latest price paid.

A PO can also be marked cancelled, and items received damaged can be logged separately to reverse the stock that was just added. You can attach files to a PO — invoices, packing slips — up to 20MB each.

Approvals

The Approvals tab (visible with purchasing_approve) is a tray of every PO currently Sent and awaiting a decision — draft POs aren't shown there since their requester hasn't submitted them yet. From the tray, or from a PO's own detail view, an approver can:

  • Approve — draw a signature to confirm; it's captured and shown on the completed PO document (see below). Approving clears any earlier rejection note.
  • Reject with a required reason — the PO drops back to Draft, the reason shows as a banner on the PO (who rejected it and why) until the requester edits it and marks it Sent again, which clears the banner.

A PO's detail view also links straight through to whatever document drove the purchase: the Purchase Request it was converted from (if any), and per line, the specific Quotation or Work Order its "Q" item traces back to — so an approver can check the original context without hunting for it separately.

Once approved, Print and Email PO on the detail view produce the same completed document — supplier, items, total, and the approver's captured signature with their name and the approval date/time.

A PO can carry the vendor's own order/quote number (e.g. an Amazon order #) separate from its own PO number — set it when creating the PO or edit it later from the PO detail view. Alongside it, a Freight Forwarding panel tracks orders that ship through a freight forwarder before reaching a branch: Pending → Received by Forwarder → Forwarded to Us, each stamped with a timestamp, plus an optional freight tracking number and notes. This status is independent of the PO's own approval/receiving status above.

Items with no product yetA PO line for something not yet in your catalog can be "linked" to a product after the fact — either an existing one or a brand-new one created on the spot — and any quantity already received retroactively applies to its stock. If the line traces back to a quotation's "Q" item, the newly created product is automatically tagged non-inventory and shows under Inventory's Non-Inventory Items tab rather than the main catalog list, since it was bought for that one quote.

Suppliers suppliers

The vendor directory purchase orders are placed against.

Standard contact/address record per supplier, plus payment terms and a flag distinguishing local from international/online suppliers. Needs suppliers_add / suppliers_edit / suppliers_delete for the respective actions.

Branch Transfers transfers

Moving stock between two of your locations.

  1. Create a transfer — stock is deducted from the source branch immediately, so it's off that branch's books as soon as the transfer exists, not just once it physically leaves.
  2. Dispatch (needs transfers_pickup) — marks it picked up from the source and in transit.
  3. Receive (needs transfers_dropoff) — add the stock to the destination branch; this can be done in partial batches over more than one delivery.

Cancelling a transfer restores whatever hadn't yet been received back to the source branch. Transfers can also be created automatically when a multi-branch quotation is accepted and part of the order has to come from another location.

The New Transfer product picker works for rental items too (tagged [Rental] in the list) — see Rentals for how a rental item's currently-checked-out units are excluded from what's offered.

Warehouse Management warehouse cycle-counts shipping

Physical storage layout, inventory audits, and outbound shipments.

Zones & bins

Set up named zones per branch, then individual storage bins within each zone (with an optional capacity). Assign products to specific bins with a quantity, marking one bin as "primary" if a product lives in more than one spot. A bin's assigned quantity is automatically kept in sync with that branch's stock level everywhere stock moves — receiving, shipping, transfers, layaway, cycle counts — so you never have to update it by hand.

Cycle Counts

A physical inventory audit. Start a session scoped to all products, a category, or a specific bin — it snapshots what the system currently expects for each item. Export the sheet to CSV for an offline count, then import the actual counted quantities back in; the system computes the variance per line. Committing the session applies only the lines that actually differ, adjusting stock and bin quantities and logging each change for audit purposes — once committed, a session is locked. Needs cyclecounts_create to run a session, cyclecounts_approve to commit it.

Shipping

Create a shipment (a set of items going out to a customer or another location), print a pick list for warehouse staff and a shipping label once it's packed, then progress it: draft → shipped (deducts stock) → delivered. A shipment can also be created directly from an online order that's ready to fulfill, and cancelling a shipment that had already shipped restores the stock it took.

Customers customers

Customer profiles, purchase history, and account status.

  • Standard contact info, loyalty points (earned automatically on every sale), and total lifetime spend.
  • Credit accounts — flag a customer as a credit customer to let them buy on account; see Accounts Receivable for how that's collected and enforced.
  • Discount card assignment (type + card number) lives on this profile — see Discount Cards.
  • Cash back card assignment (type + card number) also lives on this profile — see Cash Back Cards.
  • Rental compliance fields (ID scan, address proof, emergency reference) also live here and gate whether the customer can start a new rental.
  • A customer's full transaction history is viewable from their profile.

Needs customers_add / customers_edit / customers_delete; extending or adjusting credit terms needs customers_credit.

Accounts Receivable accounts

Managing customers who buy on credit instead of paying up front.

  • Each credit customer carries a running account balance and a credit limit; a credit sale adds to the balance instead of collecting cash.
  • Payments against the balance are recorded here (needs accounts_payments), with an aging report and a per-customer statement available.
  • Auto credit-check — every time a payment is recorded (and periodically for the whole customer list), the system checks how long a customer's oldest unpaid credit sale has been outstanding against their payment terms. Exceed it, and the account is automatically blocked from further credit sales until the balance is brought current — this happens without anyone manually flagging the account.
  • Write-offs for balances that will never be collected need accounts_writeoff.

CRM & Sales Pipeline crm

Tracking prospective business, separate from walk-in retail sales.

  • Leads (crm_leads) — a prospective contact, which can be converted into an opportunity once qualified.
  • Opportunities (crm_opportunities) — a deal in progress, moved through pipeline stages; can link directly to a quotation, so a sales-driven deal and its formal price quote stay connected.
  • Activities — calls, meetings, follow-up tasks logged against a lead or opportunity, each markable complete (or reopened). An activity can also attach straight to a customer with no lead or opportunity needed.
  • Rentals feed into a customer's Activities automatically — checkout, return, and cancellation each log a completed entry, so a rep can see a customer's rental history right in CRM. An overdue rental logs as an open (not completed) activity with the original due date, so it shows up in the Activities tab's Overdue bucket as something to follow up on — checked roughly every 30 minutes, not instantly.
  • A CRM-specific dashboard summarizes pipeline health separately from the main Dashboard.

Commissions commissions

Paying staff a cut of what they sell.

  • Define commission plans (needs commissions_plans) — a rate structure — and assign them to specific employees.
  • A commission record is created automatically whenever an assigned employee completes a sale or wins a CRM opportunity — no manual entry needed for the common case.
  • A plan's Applies To setting scopes it to POS sales, quotes, CRM deals, rentals, or all of them — a plan only earns commission on the category(ies) it's scoped to, so assigning someone a POS-only plan doesn't automatically start paying them on rentals too.
  • Rentals are commissioned on a delay, not at booking — the risk being commissioned against is different depending on how the rental was paid. A cash/card-paid rental earns commission once the item is fully returned; a credit-account rental earns commission once the customer actually pays off that rental's invoice (whether that happens before or after the item comes back). This is deliberate: it rewards staff for following up and closing the loop, not just for booking the rental.
  • Records move through approval (commissions_approve, individually or in bulk) before being marked paid (commissions_pay, also available in bulk).
  • A summary view totals commissions owed/paid across employees and time periods.

Reports & Analytics reports

Sales, inventory, and financial reporting across the business.

  • Sales — totals, trend by day, breakdown by payment method, for any date range and (optionally) a single branch.
  • Top Products and Sales by Category.
  • Inventory report — stock value (cost and retail), and the low-stock list. Warehouses are broken out separately here since they don't generate sales but still hold real stock value.
  • AR Collections — payments collected against credit accounts over a period.
  • Promotions report — usage and discount totals per promotion/code.
Why a stock number sometimes looks inconsistentA product's single overall stock figure and its per-branch stock figure are tracked in parallel, and most stock-moving actions keep both in sync — but a product that's never been touched by a branch-specific action (no branch sale, transfer, or received PO yet) may only have the overall number set. Reports that break stock down by branch and reports that use the overall figure are answering slightly different questions, which is worth knowing if two numbers ever don't match.

Exporting reports needs reports_export; financial detail reports need reports_financial.

Employees employees

Staff accounts, roles, and branch assignments.

  • Each employee has a username/password, a default branch, and a security group that determines their permissions.
  • An employee can be assigned to more than one branch (see Branches) if they need to work across locations.
  • Editing salary/pay-rate information needs employees_salaries, separate from ordinary profile edits (employees_edit).
  • employees_add / employees_delete gate creating and removing accounts.

Branches branches

Your store locations (and warehouses).

  • Each branch has its own address/contact info and, optionally, its own currency for multi-currency operations.
  • A branch can be flagged as a warehouse — a fulfillment/storage-only location excluded from sales reporting and the POS branch picker, but still tracked for stock value.
  • Assign employees to a branch (and pick one as their default) from the branch detail view.
  • Branches are never hard-deleted, only deactivated, since almost every other record in the system references one.
Multi-branch accessSeeing a branch switcher in the UI (rather than being locked to one location) depends on a separate "multi-branch access" flag on your security group, not the Branches permission itself — that permission only controls who can manage branch *records*.

Price Tier %

Each branch carries a Price Tier % — a single positive or negative percentage that recalculates every retail item's price at that branch, with no per-product setup. A branch at +10% sells everything 10% above the catalog price; a branch at -15% sells 15% below it. Leave it at 0 for a branch that should just use the flat catalog price.

Where it appliesThe tier recalculates prices at the POS register (the product grid, cart, and checkout total once a branch is selected), Quotations (a quote's line prices follow whichever branch is picked on the quote, re-filling if the branch is changed), and Reports' branch retail-value figures. It does not affect the WooCommerce/online store, which always sells at the flat catalog price. The computed price is floor-clamped at 0 (a very negative tier can't push a price below zero) and rounded to the nearest cent; the untouched catalog price is always what's edited on the product itself — the tier is a multiplier applied on top, never stored per-product.

Needs branches_add / branches_edit / branches_delete.

Security Groups security

Roles that determine what each employee can see and do.

Three groups exist out of the box:

GroupAccess
AdministratorEverything.
ManagerEverything except Settings, Branches, and Security.
CashierDashboard, POS, Customers, Transactions, Quotations, plus base Rentals/Layaway access (not the issue/cancel/payment sub-actions).

You can also create custom groups with any combination of permissions checked in the permission-tree editor. A group can't be deleted while any employee is still assigned to it — reassign them first. See the full key list in the Permission Key Reference below.

Module vs. sub-permissionChecking a whole module (e.g. "Inventory") grants everything under it. Unchecking one specific sub-permission (e.g. "Delete Products") on an otherwise-checked module denies just that one action while leaving the rest of the module enabled.

Settings & Integrations settings

Store-wide configuration.

  • Company (settings_company) — store name, logo, address, currency.
  • Tax (settings_tax) — default tax rate used where a product doesn't specify its own.
  • Payment (settings_payment) — accepted payment methods, credit terms defaults, layaway deposit percentage and cancellation fee.
  • Integrations (settings_integrations):
    • Email/SMTP — credentials for sending receipts, statements, and notifications by email.
    • Cloudinary — optional cloud image storage for product photos and PO attachments; recommended (and required on Vercel-hosted deployments, where local file storage doesn't persist).
    • WooCommerce — two-way sync of products and orders with a WooCommerce store. Once configured, the app syncs automatically on an interval you set, and you can trigger a manual sync or review the sync log here too.
Shipping carriersThe Shipping screen under Warehouse Management lets you record a carrier and tracking number per shipment freeform (UPS, FedEx, USPS, DHL, Local Delivery, Other) — there's no live carrier-rate or tracking-API integration; it's a record-keeping field, not a connected shipping service.
